## 0. Hard constraints and how they were met

- **The saying is attributed to nobody.** Four pages are quoted as their publishers' own wording. None names an originator; no first use was found.
- **Papers not read are not characterised.** Newell and Rosenbloom (1981) appears only as Heathcote et al. describe it; Soderstrom and Bjork (2015) appears only as Kliegl, Bjork and Bauml describe it. Both are flagged in the body. Gray and Lindstedt (2017) could not be obtained and is not mentioned in any copy field (see section 5).
- **No advice, no motivation, no mental-health claim.** The piece ends on what is untested. Health-adjacent sentences on the quoted pages (overtraining, illness, sleep, "see your doctor", meditation) were deliberately not reproduced.
- **No repeat of the drilling piece's literature.** Contextual interference is referred to in one clause ("this site's piece on drilling reports one case of it") and not restated; deliberate practice is not mentioned.

## 3. Contradictions

- **Power versus exponential.** Heathcote et al. find exponential functions fit individual series better. Donner and Hardy, fitting pieces, find three-parameter piecewise power laws slightly better than piecewise exponentials (90.57% vs 90.43% of variance) and did not fit Heathcote's four-parameter APEX function. Gaschler et al. find exponential better than power for most chess players and neither adequate for a substantial proportion. Recorded in `contestedNotes`; the article picks no function.
- **86 vs 85.97.** Donner and Hardy's Results give 86% for the single power law and their Discussion 85.97%. The article prints 86.
- **What a plateau is.** In Donner and Hardy a flat stretch is the tail of one power-law piece before a transition; in Lowenscuss-Erlich et al. it is defined within one session as the blocks after which successive differences are no longer significant. The article keeps the two apart by section and does not merge the definitions.

## 5. Claims cut, and why

- **Gray and Lindstedt (2017), "Plateaus, Dips, and Leaps", *Cognitive Science* 41(7).** Listed as free to read by Unpaywall, but `onlinelibrary.wiley.com` returned 403 to curl on every path tried, it is not in Europe PMC, and no repository copy was found. Not read, so not described, and not mentioned in the copy. If the owner can open it in a browser it is the obvious addition to the fourth section.
- **Soderstrom and Bjork (2015), "Learning versus performance: an integrative review".** Closed access (SAGE). Appears only as Kliegl, Bjork and Bauml cite it. No finding of the review is restated.
- **Gallistel, Fairhurst and Balsam (2004), PNAS.** Free in PMC but the publisher does not allow XML download and `pnas.org` returned 403. Cut; Donner and Hardy's description of it (abrupt steps in animal conditioning curves) was not carried into the article either, to avoid a second second-hand source.
- **Bryan and Harter (1899).** Donner and Hardy cite it for punctuated individual curves "since the 19th century". Not read; cut.
- **Stafford and Dewar (2014), online game players.** SAGE, closed; not read; cut.
- **Kesting's distinction between a slump and a plateau, and his suggested causes** (training volume, illness, overtraining). Cut: health-adjacent and advice.
- **Any sentence about sleep or consolidation** in the Lowenscuss-Erlich paper. The article says "24 to 48 hours later" and nothing about why.
- **Donner and Hardy's suggestion that training could be planned around transitions,** and their age and education associations. Cut: the first is advice, the second ranks people.
- **Any statement that plateaus in grappling are caused by, or ended by, anything.** No source.
