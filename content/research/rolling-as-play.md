## 0. Hard constraints and how they were met

- **No advice; no developmental, health, mental-health or safety claim.** The play-fighting literature and the martial arts paper both argue that play does something for development. None of that is reproduced; the article says twice that it is left out. Safety wording on the gym pages ("tap early", "slow and controlled", injury prevention) is not quoted.
- **Huizinga and Caillois not characterised from memory.** Huizinga appears only as Thomas Henricks (2008) summarises him, and the article says Homo Ludens was not read. Caillois was cut entirely (section 7).
- **The animal literature is not used as evidence about people.** The final section says so, and names the species.
- **"No study tests it" is stated as what was found**, in one day's searching (section 2, "Searches run").

## 3. Contradictions and how each was handled

- **50:50 or not.** The older rule says partners win equally; the Pellis reviews say reciprocity is needed but can be uneven (role reversals of about 25-30% in rats, 30% in pigs). Both are printed, in that order.
- **Is pig play fighting play at all?** The 2017 review concedes the warty-pig encounters "could be argued" to be aggressive and classifies them as play on four criteria. The article reports the third strategy without that debate, and the contested notes record that all figures are second hand. If space allows, the caveat is worth a clause.
- **"Play fighting" or "rough-and-tumble play".** The 2024 review says some authors prefer the second because it stresses cooperation; the 2022 paper uses it for that reason. The article uses play fighting for animals and rough-and-tumble play only where the Swedish paper does.
- **Play or training.** Impact's sheet says rounds are "training, not fighting" and "learning"; it never calls them play (its only uses of the word are "Do not ... play with railing" and "Empty mats are not play areas", both about children and fixtures). The article does not claim any gym calls a round play; the vocabulary claim rests on "play guard" alone.
- **Blomqvist Mickelsson and Stylin on age.** They write that "It is a rare sight to see two adults wrestle for the fun of it at social events" and that rough-and-tumble play declines with age. Verbatim, relevant, cut for length.
- **Channon's criticism of O'Shea** (that she calls sport fights "simulated" versions of real ones while arguing they are a different thing) is not reproduced; the book was not read.

## 7. Claims cut and why

- *Cut:* "grapplers say they play jiu-jitsu". Only "play guard", "guard player" and "game" were found in a source read today.
- *Cut:* anything about Portuguese (*jogar*) or the origin of the usage. Not sourced.
- *Cut:* Caillois altogether, and any typology of games. One second-hand sentence was available and it went in the trim.
- *Cut:* every developmental or functional claim: that play fighting trains socio-cognitive skills, executive function or emotional regulation (both reviews); that fair play teaches fairness (Schank et al.); that sparring builds empathy, prosocial behaviour or friendship (Blomqvist Mickelsson & Stylin); O'Shea's political claims as Channon reports them.
- *Cut:* that play fighting is pleasurable or rewarding (2022 and 2017 papers). True to the sources, but about rats' reward systems, and one step from a claim about how a round feels.
- *Cut:* the criteria for telling play from serious fighting, one of which is that no combat-induced injuries occurred. Too easily read as a statement about injury in sparring.
- *Cut:* "people agree their limits in words and in writing, which no animal in the reviews does". Ours, reasonable, unsourced, and the reviews do describe play signals; removed in the trim.
- *Cut:* an earlier line that more experienced grapplers routinely work from the bottom or give positions away to partners. Common knowledge in gyms and not in any document read; replaced by the statement that role reversal "is harder to find in print".
- *Cut:* Impact's location (Oregon). Not on the sheet; the tap piece states it.
- *Cut:* the cross-reference to the ego piece inside the body ("No one is keeping track of points" as the request that piece read into the saying). Went in the trim; the piece is still in `relatedSlugs`.

## 8. Editorial notes

- The order is deliberate, as in the flow piece: vocabulary, the reported definition, the animal literature and its limits, the rooms' own documents, the one paper that connects them, and only then the argument.
- The strongest single point is strategy (3) in the 2017 review, "cease and desist if the loser signals submission", beside the tap. The article lets it stand in one sentence. It is still pigs.
- "Self-handicapping" is the reviewers' term (2017 heading) and the Swedish paper's; the article never applies it to a named person or room in its own voice outside the argument section.
- Consistency: the tap, flow and leverage pieces are referred to in the body; the ego and drilling pieces are in `relatedSlugs` only. All five are registered in `index.ts`. No literature from those pieces is repeated.
- Body copy is 1,795 words, five under the ceiling. Any restoration needs a matching cut.
- Not registered: `src/content/journal/index.ts` was not edited. The export is `rollingAsPlay`.
