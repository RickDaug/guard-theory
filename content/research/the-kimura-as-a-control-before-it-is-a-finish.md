### Naming

The technique is given three names — *ude-garami* (judo nomenclature), double wristlock (catch wrestling), kimura (most jiu-jitsu rooms). **No individual is credited with inventing it and no account of where the third name came from is printed.** The article says the story is told in several versions and declines to adjudicate. This was a deliberate decision, not a gap: the accounts in circulation depend on a single 1951 match, and printing them would require stating a competition result, which the commission forbids.

## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **The name.** Multiple incompatible versions in circulation, all resting on one match. None printed. See §1.
- **Whether the grip is primarily a control.** This is a live coaching disagreement and the article owns it as a position rather than presenting it as consensus. The final section explicitly invites the reader to falsify it.
- **Legality of shoulder locks by division.** UWW's junior restrictions do not mention shoulder locks; the IBJJF's table restricts the omoplata and the wrist lock for some divisions. The article reports both without inferring a coherent policy across organisations, because there is not one.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following cut:**

- *Cut:* the whole account of the technique's English name and the 1951 match behind it. Requires a competition result, which the commission prohibits, and the versions in circulation disagree.
- *Cut:* every physiological description of what a rotational shoulder lock does. Risk is described as a short rotational range, body weight travelling through it faster than a person can decide, and the fact that it is applied where the recipient cannot see it.
- *Cut:* the claim that the kimura is "the most versatile grip in jiu-jitsu" or similar. Unsourceable superlative.
- *Cut:* any statistic on kimura frequency in competition. None exists in anything consulted.
- *Cut:* every division assignment from the IBJJF illegal-moves table. The grid's per-column marks are images and do not extract as text; only the row captions do. Named as appearing in the table, no more.
- *Cut:* a planned claim that UWW *permits* shoulder locks for U13/U15. The document lists what is restricted; it does not enumerate what is permitted. The article says the restriction list does not include them, which is what the source supports.
- *Cut:* the assertion that catch wrestling and judo "independently developed" the same grip. Plausible; unsourced; removed. The article says three traditions used it differently, which is a weaker and defensible statement.

## 10. Editorial notes

- The three set-up sections are deliberately unequal in length. Half guard and side control get mechanics; the front headlock section is short because it exists to make the pattern visible, and it hands off to the guillotine article.
- The risk material is split across §4 (the sweep rotation) and §5 (the step-over finish) rather than pooled, because the two set-ups fail differently. Do not merge them into one safety paragraph.
- §7 ends by telling the reader to test the thesis themselves and to accept a negative result. That is the intended tone. It is not an invitation to add a conclusion.
