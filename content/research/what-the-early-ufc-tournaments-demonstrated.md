## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

There is no source-versus-source contradiction in this piece, because the article declines to enter the territory where the contradictions live. Event dates, bracket sizes, entrant lists and bout results circulate in many versions; none is stated. The article's method is to describe the format only through a regulator's characterisation of the sport as a whole, which is a weaker claim that is fully sourced.

The one interpretive tension, stated in the text: the promotion's own record book excludes the events the popular argument rests on. The article treats this as the promotion's reasonable editorial decision and as a reason for readers to be cautious, not as evidence of bad faith.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* every bout result, method, time and competitor name from the early events. None could be verified against a primary or federation record, and the promotion's own record book excludes those events.
- *Cut:* bracket size. The commonly stated eight-competitor bracket could not be sourced to a primary document in this pass, so the article says only "a small bracket" and "several matches on the same evening", the latter quoted from the regulator. An earlier draft's phrase "an eight-person or four-person bracket" was removed for this reason.
- *Cut:* event dates and event numbering.
- *Cut:* "there were no rules". False, and the article says so implicitly by describing what did and did not exist; the regulator's list is of *absent* rules, not of no rules.
- *Cut:* any claim about which discipline performed better, and any style-versus-style comparison.
- *Cut:* the 1996–97 political and broadcast history (Senate criticism, cable removals, state bans). Real, but nothing in it could be sourced to a primary document this pass, and the argument does not need it.
- *Cut:* any injury-risk claim or medical statement.
- *Cut:* everything from the 2009 ABC report, which is a scanned PDF with no extractable text.

## 10. Editorial notes

- The design decision that makes this piece work: **argue the format from a regulator's description rather than from event records.** It is the only way to write about these events without either citing a fan wiki or repeating a result.
- The article deliberately concedes a great deal to the popular argument in section 6. A piece that only debunks would be less useful and less honest; the narrow claim it defends is genuinely substantial.
- Watch for overlap with `why-sport-jiu-jitsu-does-not-transfer-directly-to-mma`, which uses the ABC rules and NAC 467 for the fence, the clock and the scorecard. This piece uses them for the tournament format and the 24-hour cap. Neither should restate the other.
- If a primary source for the bracket structure of the early events is found — a commission filing, a contemporary press report — the format section can be tightened. Until then, "several matches on the same evening" is what the record supports.
