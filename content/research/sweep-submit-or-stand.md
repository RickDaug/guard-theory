## 0. Hard constraints applied

- No medical, injury-prevention, safety or self-defence advice. Kesting's 2009 article *Getting Back to Your Feet From Guard* is about MMA and street fighting; only its list of three options is quoted, and the article says once that the setting is not taken up. His injury caveat in *Refusing to Concede the Sweep* was read and not used.
- No invented statistic, no ranking, no unverified record. Kesting's guess that the omoplata "only results in a submission about one third of the time" was read and deliberately not used. The glossary's remarks about named competitors (Marcelo Garcia at ADCC 2003, BJ Penn at UFC 63, etc.) were not used.
- Consistency with `the-technical-stand-up`, `closed-guard-and-what-the-locked-ankles-buy`, `seated-guard-and-supine-guard`, `why-the-underhook-decides-half-guard`, `guard-retention-as-a-system` and the Library entries `sweeping-toward-the-missing-post`, `connection-in-open-guard`, `retention-ladder-frame-angle-invert-recover`. - The framing is labelled. The five categories and the sweep/submit/get-up sentence are Kesting's; "exits", retention as "a job that is not an exit", the filing of the back take and the test are ours and are in `contestedNotes`.

## 3. Contradictions and wrinkles

- **Retention, two definitions.** BJJ Mental Models' retention phase is the moment the guard "has essentially been passed". The Journal's retention piece uses the word for the continuous management of distance, angle and connection. The article quotes the former and paraphrases the latter without reconciling them; both describe not leaving.
- **Back take.** IBJJF 4.6.2 calls one version a sweep; 4.5 scores back control separately at four points. Recorded in contestedNotes.
- **Standing up.** Kesting lists it as a category in its own right and also calls stand-up-then-takedown a "sweep" in quotation marks; IBJJF 4.6.3 files it under Sweep. The article states this, it does not resolve it.
- **Stalling from guard.** 6.2.1 D's list of exempt defensive positions omits guard. The article states the omission and makes no prediction about refereeing.
- **ADCC and coming to the feet.** The page has no clause on it (full-text search for feet / stand up / get up returns nothing relevant), which is why paragraph three of section two confines itself to "the IBJJF scoreboard". The stand-up piece covers the ADCC reading.

- Any statement that most beginners learn guard as a list of techniques: softened to "the usual answer", no count claimed.
- "The two rule books most no-gi competitors meet": unsourced, cut.
- Any claim that one exit is higher percentage, safer or better for a body type.
- Half guard history from Glossary Part 3 ("Years ago it was seen as a pitiful position"): history, not needed, not verified.
- Named competitors and results in the glossary.
- BJJ Mental Models' gi/no-gi claim about supine guards: outside this piece and already near the seated/supine article.
- Kesting's three stages of passing (*The Unpassable Guard*): read for the retention section, cut for length.
