## 1. Core claims made in the article

| # | Claim | Support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | A rash guard is a synthetic filament fabric (usually polyester) with a smaller percentage of elastane, and the two fail differently: the polyester abrades and pills, the elastane loses return. | Materials characterisation is general and uncontroversial; the failure-mode distinction is supported by the two elastane studies below, which measure *recovery* rather than strength. No source is claimed for "usually polyester" beyond it being the ordinary construction; the article does not attach a number to it. |
| A2 | Jovanović et al. (2022) knitted plated weft fabrics from polyamide with elastane percentages from 0% to 43%, cyclically loaded them, and calculated unrecovered elongation, elastic elongation and a hysteresis index. Result: elastane percentage "significantly affects the size of the elastic region of the knitted fabric and has no effect on the hysteresis index"; conclusion, optimise the percentage per fabric design. | Abstract read directly on PMC9570736. |
| A3 | Sülar & Öner (2019) laundered 12 elastane knitted fabrics per ISO 6330 in a home-type front-loading machine at 40 °C (1 h) with a non-phosphate detergent, at 0, 5, 15 and 25 cycles, measuring residual extension at four recovery times (0 min, 2 min, 30 min, 24 h) by the M&S P15A method. Effect of repeated washing is "gradual"; critical washing cycles were 5 and 15; ground yarn and gimped elastane yarn construction changed residual extension. | Full PDF downloaded and read (`pdftotext`), not a summary. |
| A4 | That paper reports, citing Lau et al., that resilience values decreased by 10–20% after sixteen washing cycles. | Read in the discussion of the Sülar & Öner PDF. **Attributed in the article as their citation of earlier work, not as their own result.** |
| A5 | Fibers and Polymers (2024) subjected warp-knitted swimwear fabric to artificial weathering for 120 and 240 h; UV was "the main detrimental parameter"; breaking force, elongation at break, fabric elasticity and stiffness, and air permeability were "dramatically influenced"; SEM showed micro-damage; FTIR showed no chemical destruction; seawater effect "minimal or not distinctive". | Abstract read directly on the Springer article page. |
| A6 | Callewaert et al. (2014): T-shirts from 26 individuals after an intensive bicycle spinning session, incubated 28 h; trained odour panel found significant differences between polyester and cotton on hedonic value, intensity and five qualitative characteristics; polyester smelled less pleasant and more intense; micrococci in almost all synthetic shirts, staphylococci abundant on both. | PubMed abstract read directly; PMC4249026. |
| A7 | GINETEX: the five care symbols are protected trademarks, GINETEX describes itself as the origin of the ISO 3758 standard, symbols indicate the *maximum permitted treatment*, gentler treatment is always permitted, and "care labelling is not a guarantee of quality". ISO 3758:2023 (fourth edition) published December 2023, replacing ISO 3758:2012. | GINETEX care-labelling page, read directly. |
| A8 | ASTM D2594/D2594M-21 covers stretch properties of knitted fabrics having low power and measures fabric stretch and fabric growth. | ASTM standard page (carried forward from `how-a-bjj-rash-guard-should-fit`, re-verified: resolves to store.astm.org, HTTP 200). |
| A9 | AATCC maintains test methods including dimensional change and colourfastness in laundering. | aatcc.org/testing, HTTP 200. Specific method numbers deliberately **not** cited — see cuts. |

## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

No two sources here contradict each other; the problem in this subject is absence rather than conflict. The article says so directly and devotes a whole section to the four claims that could not be sourced. That section is the honest counterpart of a "contradictory accounts" section for this piece.

One tension is named in the text: the near-universal advice to line-dry rather than tumble-dry is unsupported in one direction and complicated in the other by the UV finding. The article states both and issues no rule.

## 7. Image requirements

- A cross-section diagram of a plated knit showing the two fibre systems, with the elastane identified as the smaller component. Must be labelled in a real key beneath, per house rules on diagrams.
- A plate of the five GINETEX symbols **only if licensing is cleared** — see rights. Otherwise, describe them.
- A simple chart of the concept the evidence supports: recovery declining gradually across wash cycles. It must be drawn as an unlabelled trend, not with numbers, because the one study that measured it used a different fabric.
- **No before/after photographs of garments.** We have no test data of our own and a staged photograph would imply we do.

## 8. Rights concerns

- **GINETEX symbols are registered trademarks** and the organisation states that licensees are bound by an intellectual-property agreement and that improper use may lead to legal action. **Do not reproduce the symbols without clearing it.** The article's text references them without depiction, which is safe.
- **ISO and ASTM standards are copyrighted.** Only the scope and the designation are described; no clause is reproduced.
- The Sülar & Öner PDF is hosted by the publisher and the paper is cited, not reproduced.
- No product, brand or competitor is named anywhere in the article, deliberately.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* a maximum wash temperature. No study located that tested wash temperature against elastic recovery in this fabric class; the article states this as a gap.
- *Cut:* detergent chemistry (alkalinity, enzymes, "gentle"). The one laundering study used a single non-phosphate detergent and did not compare formulations.
- *Cut:* chlorine. The Epps 1987 paper covering light, sea water and chlorine could not be read this pass (SAGE 403). Stated as a gap; flagged for reinstatement if the paper can be obtained.
- *Cut:* fabric softener, in both directions. No source.
- *Cut:* the entire tumble-dryer instruction as a rule. Retained only as a named absence.
- *Cut:* specific AATCC method numbers. The brief warns against citing a method number from memory, and the individual method pages could not be opened in this pass (`members.aatcc.org` timed out). The article refers to the methods by what they cover, not by number.
- *Cut:* any lifespan figure for any garment, including ours (owner-decision item 3 is unresolved).
- *Cut:* every hygiene, odour-removal, antimicrobial and infection claim. The odour study is reported as an odour-panel and microbiology finding, and the article states explicitly that it makes no hygiene claim.
- *Cut:* any statement of the polyester/elastane blend ratio of a typical rash guard. No source, and it varies.

## 10. Editorial notes

- The structural decision that carries the piece: **do not write a list of rules.** Every published version of this article elsewhere is a numbered list, and every item on those lists is one of the four things we could not source.
- The "what we could not source" section is the most valuable part and should not be trimmed for length. If the piece has to be cut, cut the fibre-primer section.
- The polyamide/viscose versus polyester mismatch must stay visible. It is the single most load-bearing caveat in the piece and it would be very easy to lose in an edit.
- If the Epps 1987 paper is obtained, add a chlorine section; do not add one from any other kind of source.
- Consistency check against `how-a-bjj-rash-guard-should-fit`: that article already uses Jovanović and ASTM D2594. The two must not describe the same study differently. The fit article uses it for *stretch versus recovery*; this one uses it for *which component fails*. That division should be maintained.
