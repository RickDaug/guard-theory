## 1. Core claims made in the article

### Treated as contested (stated as contested in the body **and** in `contestedNotes`)

| # | Claim | Positions found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Date and place of first appearance in Brazil. | NDL: port of Santos, 1914. Cairus: 1914, travelling via Argentina and Uruguay. Drysdale: official first appearance 25 September 1914 in São Paulo, explicitly rejecting the widely circulated 14 November 1914 Porto Alegre date, with a possible unconfirmed July appearance. Belém: NDL 1915, Cairus "late 1915", Drysdale "sometime in October 1915". |
| B2 | Fight record. | NDL repeats that in judogi matches he won all 1,000 without a loss; other retellings say 2,000. Pedreira: verifiable legitimate contests "could be counted on three or four hands and even some of those are questionable"; most were exhibitions or professional-wrestling appearances. |
| B3 | Whether Carlos Gracie was taught by Maeda personally. | Cairus: apprenticeship "under Mayeda, and his local senior student Jacyntho Ferro", beginning 1916, "lasted no more than three years". Drysdale: Ferro "seems to be the likely source of Carlos Gracie's grappling knowledge", explicitly not claiming they never met. Pedreira: "There is no evidence that Carlos ever met Maeda." |
| B4 | What Maeda conferred on Brazilian students. | Cairus: in 1928 Maeda told Hajime Otake he had never awarded a black belt to any student in Brazil. Drysdale: five Brazilians were promoted to a rank the press recorded as *primeiro galão*, whose modern equivalent is unknown — near-brown, first dan, or a scheme of Maeda's own. |
| B5 | Whether Maeda taught "judo" or "jiu-jitsu". | Pedreira: "Maeda was not a jiu-jitsu practitioner. Maeda was a Kodokan judoka." Drysdale agrees and notes jiu-jitsu was simply the period's popular term. The article states this rather than using the terms interchangeably. |
| B6 | Whether Maeda was a student of, or an emissary sent by, Kano Jigoro. | Drysdale: "he was never a student of Jigoro Kano." The emissary framing is treated in the article as a later tidying-up. |
| B7 | The Luiz França → Oswaldo Fadda line. | BJJ Heroes describes França as having learned from Maeda (and Satake and Omori), noting the assertion "is disputed by some", and describes Fadda as founding the most successful non-Gracie lineage. Robert Drysdale has publicly questioned whether that lineage is properly called non-Gracie. Presented as unresolved. |

## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **B1 arrival dates.** Not reconciled. The article prints all three and uses the disagreement to make its methodological point.
- **B2 fight record.** Not reconciled; the article explains *why* the two accounts are not really in competition (an advertised record is a marketing document, not a scorecard) without asserting that either source is wrong about what it says.
- **B3 who taught Carlos.** Not reconciled. All three positions are attributed by name, and the article states which is supported by all three (Ferro) and which rests on a 65-year-old self-report (direct instruction by Maeda).
- **B4 ranks.** The article argues both accounts can be simultaneously true and says so.
- **B7 França/Fadda.** Explicitly marked as documented less carefully than it deserves. No claim is made about which side is right.

## 5. Names verified

- **Mitsuyo Maeda** (前田 光世) — NDL. "Conde Koma" per NDL. Rendered "Mayeda" in Cairus, which follows his sources; the article uses Maeda throughout and this note records the variant.
- **Kano Jigoro** — founder of the Kodokan, NDL.
- **Carlos Gracie**, **Gastão Gracie** — Cairus.
- **Jacyntho Ferro** — spelling per both Cairus and Drysdale.
- **Waldemar Lopes, Raphael Gomes, Jacyntho Ferro, Dr Matheus Pereira, Guilherme de la Rocque** — the five named by Drysdale, transcribed from his interview answer.
- **Hajime Otake** — Cairus.
- **Luiz França Filho**, **Oswaldo Fadda** — BJJ Heroes.
- **José Cairus**, **Roberto Pedreira**, **Robert Drysdale** — named researchers, spelling per their published work.

Diacritics are dropped in the article body (Belem, Gastao, Jose, primeiro galao) to match the ASCII convention of the existing content files. They are correct in this file.

## 6. Quotations approved

1. **Carlos Gracie**, as quoted by Cairus from an interview given sixty-five years later: "I was physically frail and had an inferiority complex, but I learnt the secrets of jiu-jitsu under the paternal supervision of the Japanese master." — Verified verbatim against the Cairus text, including its surrounding sentence ("The Japanese arrived in Belem. Soon afterwards, they showcased a presentation at Teatro da Paz…"). Attributed in the article as a recollection reported by Cairus, with its age stated.

Other researchers' positions (Pedreira, Drysdale) are **paraphrased and attributed**, not quoted, except for short phrases whose exact wording is reproduced in this file for checking:
- Pedreira: "There is no evidence that Carlos ever met Maeda." / "Maeda was not a jiu-jitsu practitioner. Maeda was a Kodokan judoka."
- Drysdale: "Jacyntho Ferro seems to be the likely source of Carlos Gracie's grappling knowledge." / "he was never a student of Jigoro Kano." / "The only certain issue is that Maeda promoted these men…"

**No quotation is attributed to Maeda himself.** None was found in a source good enough to use.

## 8. Rights concerns

**This is the highest-rights-risk article of the three.**

- **Living persons and family reputation.** The article touches a claim central to a living family's public identity. Every contested statement is attributed to a named published researcher, phrased as that researcher's position, and paired with the position that contradicts it. No motive is imputed to any individual, living or dead. The sentence "the person with the most to gain from it" refers to the structural weakness of a self-report and is about evidence, not about honesty; **if legal review objects to it, cut it — the paragraph survives without it.**
- **Photographs.** Any Maeda image must have its public-domain status independently established. Japanese and Brazilian copyright terms differ; do not rely on a Wikipedia licence tag alone.
- **Quotation.** The one direct quotation is short, attributed, and used for reporting and criticism.
- **BJJ Heroes** content is used for the França/Fadda lineage only, is credited, and is explicitly flagged in the article body as less well documented.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* that Kano Jigoro sent Maeda abroad to spread judo. Contradicted by Drysdale; no source supports it at the standard required.
- *Cut:* that Maeda personally awarded Carlos Gracie any rank. No source supports it and Cairus reports the opposite.
- *Cut:* Donato Pires dos Reis's reported 1931 public denial, in the Brazilian press, that Carlos Gracie was a direct pupil of Maeda. **This is genuinely useful contemporary evidence and it was cut only because it could not be traced past a secondary summary of Serrano and Pedreira.** It should be reinstated if the primary newspaper citation or the page reference in *Choque* / *O Livro Proibido do Jiu-Jítsu* can be supplied.
- *Cut:* the total number of Maeda's contests (any figure). "A handful" is used because Pedreira's "three or four hands" is itself an estimate.
- *Cut:* Maeda's naturalisation, his posthumous dan grade and the Tomé-Açu settlement date — all traceable only to Wikipedia in this pass.
- *Cut:* Drysdale's remarks about Maeda's personal habits (gambling, drinking, dress). Characterisation of a dead man's private life, not needed by the argument.

## 10. Editorial notes

- The article's structure is deliberately anti-narrative: agreed facts first, then each disputed claim as its own section. That ordering is the editorial position, and rewriting it into a chronological story would undo the point.
- The closing section gives the reader three transferable tests rather than a moral. It should not be replaced with a summary of the article.
- **Do not add a lineage diagram.** Every such diagram published elsewhere resolves exactly the questions this article says are unresolved.
- If the Journal ever publishes a Gracie-family profile, it must be consistent with this piece or explain why it differs.
