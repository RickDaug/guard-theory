## 0. Hard constraints

- No medical, injury-prevention, safety or self-defence advice. The article contains none. Pressure is described only as where weight goes. Several sources describe pressure in terms of breathing, the diaphragm, the face or pain (Kesting on kesa gatame, reverse mount and the crossface; BJJ Mental Models' "suffocating chest to face pin"); none of that was carried over. See section 6.
- No invented statistic, no ranking of people, no competition result. The only numbers are counts read off a source: seventeen further variations in the Grapplearts guide (it numbers eighteen, the first being the basic form), eight then three points of mat contact in Kesting's 2006 piece, ten osaekomi-waza in the Kodokan list.
- Consistency with `escaping-side-control`, `frames-versus-blocks`, `mount-is-a-hip-pin` and `positional-hierarchy`. The escape order used (frame, bridge, hip escape, knee) and the two doors are the Library's. One Library sentence is quoted ("Choose the door by their weight"); the rest is referred to, not restated.

## 3. Contradictions and gaps — how each was handled

- **Who wrote the Grapplearts guide.** The page's byline, its `author` meta tag and its schema all say Mark Lovegrove. The text is in the first person ("Here's a video I shot"), ends "Good luck with your future training! Stephan Kesting", and refers to Kesting's own products as "that I put together". The article cites it as "the Grapplearts guide", names both people once, and records the point in `contestedNotes`. No sentence from it is attributed to Kesting alone.
- **One position or several.** Kesting calls north-south "a form of side control" and files kesa gatame as a separate "unorthodox" position; the guide numbers north-south and two kesa forms as variations 11, 13 and 15 of side control; the Kodokan lists each hold as its own technique. The article prints the disagreement as the subject of its sixth section and does not resolve it.
- **Judo names against no-gi positions.** The Kodokan definitions specify collar and belt grips (kesa-gatame, kami-shiho-gatame, yoko-shiho-gatame). The article says so, and `contestedNotes` says none is identical to a no-gi position. The guide's own wording is hedged ("essentially the same position") and is quoted with the hedge.
- **Spelling.** The guide writes "kuzure kesa gatame" and "ushiro kesa gatame"; the Kodokan hyphenates. Each is printed as its source prints it: unhyphenated inside the guide's quotations, hyphenated where the Kodokan list is being described.
- **"Crossface" comes from wrestling.** Stated by the guide only. Printed as what the guide says.
- **Biceps or shoulder.** The guide says the crossface in side control uses the shoulder; Kesting's escape article says the face is turned "with his biceps area". The article follows the guide for the top view and does not quote the biceps wording.
- **Pin durations.** Kesting gives 25 seconds for a judo pin in one article and 20 seconds in another. Neither figure is used; `what-counts-as-a-pin` covers the judo clock from the IJF rules.
- **Chest to chest.** The guide says "Typically but not always". The article quotes it and does not treat chest-to-chest as a requirement.

## 6. Claims cut, and why

- **"Reverse side control" / "twister side control" as names.** The brief's examples. Neither phrase appears in any page read today (the guide calls the arrangement "Hips Fully Turned Towards Feet" and gives only the judo name). Not printed. The position itself is covered under the guide's description.
- **"Hundred kilos" as a translation of a Portuguese term.** Kesting prints "100 kilos" as an alias and gives no origin. The origin is not stated.
- **Anything about breathing, the diaphragm, the face or pain.** Present in Kesting's kesa gatame, reverse mount, crossface and escape articles and in BJJ Mental Models' *Head Position*. Cut under the no-medical, no-safety rule; pressure is described only as where weight goes.
- **The Library's safety notes on the crossface.** Not restated or summarised; the top comment says the Library carries them.
- **Submissions and transitions from side control** (kimura, armbar, north-south choke, mount, back take as technique). Out of scope; the back take is mentioned only as what the guide says the turn away offers.
- **Judo and wrestling pin durations** (Kesting: 3 seconds, 20 or 25 seconds). Inconsistent between his own articles and already covered from the rule books in `what-counts-as-a-pin`.
- **Any claim about which variation holds best, or for which body type.** The guide declines to rank them beyond "start with the first"; no ranking is printed.
- **A match between scarf hold and "modified scarf hold" / makura-kesa-gatame.** Reported to Kesting by two readers; not in the Kodokan list. Left out.
- **The IBJJF definition of guard** (a leg between the bodies ends the pin). Already quoted in `the-bridge-and-the-hip-escape`; the rule book was not re-read today, so nothing from it is cited or quoted here. The article says only that the Library counts the escape finished when the knee arrives.
- **Content of the instructional videos embedded in the Grapplearts pages.** Only written text was used.
