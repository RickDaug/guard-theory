## 0. Hard constraints and how they were met

- **The saying is attributed to nobody.** No page read names an author, a first use or a date. The one page that mentions an association (Breaking Muscle) names a family, not a person, and gives no source; the article paraphrases that as "one well-known family of the art" and does not print the name (see section 6).
- **No advice, no medical or mental-health claim.** The psychology literature is reported as what two reviews and one study found, with the limits their authors state. The article says twice that nothing in it is advice, and repeats the house line from `what-a-tap-is` that it gives no advice about when to tap.
- **No invented statistic, no "most gyms".** Four academies are described as four academies.

## 3. Contradictions and how each was handled

- **The rooms disagree with each other.** Three write the idea down as a rule; Dark Path publishes an objection to the phrase. Both are quoted, and the piece ends closer to the objection than to the sentence.
- **The two questionnaires disagree.** Lochbaum 2016a found the TEOSQ and POSQ gave different means and supported different hypotheses (elite athletes; individual versus team sport). The article prints the disagreement instead of picking an instrument.
- **Ego orientation: maladaptive or not.** 2016a says ego has "historically been paired with maladaptive or less desirable" outcomes; 2016b finds the relationships small and the negative impact "minimal". Both the .23 with undesirable behaviours and the positive .10 and .14 are printed.
- **Brevig et al. versus earlier work.** The paper says its finding of similar goal orientations in women and men contradicts Albuquerque et al. (2015). The sex comparison is not the article's subject and the contradiction is not restated; only the means are given.
- **The word "interdependence".** 2016a uses "interdependence" for what the reported statistic shows as near-independence (rw = .18; "True interdependence would be a correlation of 0"). The article avoids the word and says a high score on one says little about the other.

## 7. Claims cut and why

- *Cut:* any origin story, first use or named originator of the saying. None was found; the brief forbids attributing it to a person.
- *Cut:* "said to people on their first day" and every other frequency claim about the phrase ("most gyms", "every academy"). No count exists. The newcomers point is carried only as Breaking Muscle's quoted description.
- *Cut:* any statement that ego orientation leads to injury, refusal to tap, burnout, anxiety or quitting. Nothing read supports it for jiu-jitsu, and the first two would be safety claims.
- *Cut:* any statement that task orientation makes people better at jiu-jitsu or keeps them training. The reviews are correlational and the BJJ study cannot give direction.
- *Cut:* Castro-Sanchez et al. (2019), reported in Brevig as finding ego orientation more prominent as judo competition level rose. Relevant to the counterpoint, but second hand and not read.
- *Cut:* the elite rows of Lochbaum 2016a Tables 4 and 5 as numbers. The layout extraction did not align sport-level row labels with values; only the authors' prose conclusion (A18) is used.
- *Cut:* the response scales of the TEOSQ and POSQ, and the POSQ items listed in the Ambroży paper.
- *Cut:* the 2 x 2 and 3 x 2 extensions of the theory, except as needed to name the scores in the BJJ study.
- *Cut:* "Nothing is recorded when it happens" (of a tap in training). Unsourced generalisation about rooms.
- *Cut:* a sentence that ranks are awarded by someone who has watched how a student does against others. Plausible, unsourced today.
- *Cut:* anything on the Freudian sense of ego. Not read, not needed.

## 8. Editorial notes

- The section order matters: where it is written, what the term means, what was found, the one jiu-jitsu sample, then the site's reading, then the counterpoint. Moving the reading earlier would let it borrow the authority of the research.
- Every restatement of the Brevig study carries at least one of its qualifiers (women, currently training, cross-sectional, self-report). Keep it that way.
- The piece ends unsettled on purpose. If a study of the saying itself, or of goal orientation and persistence in grappling, appears, the last section needs rewriting.
- Consistency: `what-a-tap-is` (no advice on when to tap; no referee in a training round), the drilling piece ("whether the round is treated as a verdict"), the dropout piece (nobody can say who left). All three are in `relatedSlugs` and all three are registered in `index.ts`.
- Not registered: `src/content/journal/index.ts` was not edited. The export is `leaveYourEgoAtTheDoor`.
