## 0. The brief's hard constraints

No self-defence advice, no claim about what works in a real assault, no crime statistics, no fight results. The article describes what organisations say and what rules reward. ## 3. Contradictions and how handled

- **The brief asked for "the IBJJF's own description of its purpose".** No such page was found on ibjjf.com. The article says so, limits the statement to the pages read, and reports what the graduation page and rule book do say. The limit is recorded in `contestedNotes`.
- **The ADCC does not sit cleanly in the sport camp.** Its about page names self-defence as a goal of the club and describes the championship as a sport without strikes. Printed as two stories from one page, not reconciled.
- **IBJJF version label.** The download page calls the book "v6.0"; the PDF footers read "VERSION 6.1 2024". The source title records both (same handling as `what-a-tap-is`).
- **Spelling.** Our copy uses "self-defence"; Gracie University writes "self-defense", the ADCC "self defense", the UKBJJA "self defence". Quotations keep each source's form.
- **Existing site position.** The MMA transfer article's "skill is priced by a ruleset" is cited by name and extended, not restated; the points tables are summarised in two sentences and left to `position-before-submission-and-what-the-points-table-teaches` and `how-no-gi-rulesets-reshaped-technique-selection`. Nothing from the early-UFC article is repeated; it is linked because it treats the event both camps appeal to.

## 5. Claims cut, and why

- *Cut:* Gracie University's statement that studies of real fights by the family show 36 techniques used more often and with greater success than all others. An efficacy claim about real fights; only the count of 36 is reported.
- *Cut:* "learning skills that could actually save their lives", "injuries are extremely rare", and the page's account of why beginners quit. Efficacy, safety and dropout claims; none is repeated.
- *Cut:* the About page's account of the UFC and of results in it, and the course pages' percentage claims about street fights. Fight results and unsourced statistics.
- *Cut:* the U.S. Army origin of the course. True to the page but unverified against any Army source, and not needed.
- *Cut:* any named individual, including the ADCC's founder and the Gracie University instructors. The ADCC history is given as "the page says".
- *Cut:* "the oldest argument in the art". Unsourceable; the opening says only that the answers sort into two camps.
- *Cut:* the IBJJF academy-naming rule that rejects names implying "a different martial art or sport" (its examples include "Jiu-Jitsu Tradicional"). Verified on the Academy Registration page, but reading it as a position on this argument would be over-interpretation.
- *Cut:* the June 2026 graduation change tying black belt at 18 to an Adult World Championship title. Verified, off the point.
- *Cut:* any statement of what "most rooms" teach or how many rooms belong to either camp.
- *Not stated:* that the IBJJF has no mission statement anywhere. Only that none was found on the pages read.

## 6. Editorial notes

- The opening section's scope paragraph is load-bearing; keep it.
- Section 6's four questions must stay questions about a room's practice. If an editor wants them firmer, they must not become advice about personal safety or a recommendation of either model.
- "Rehearse against a scripted, untrained attacker and that is the problem you become fluent in" is the sentence closest to an efficacy claim. It is inside the section labelled as argument and is about fluency in a practised problem, not about outcomes outside the room. An editor may prefer to soften it further.
