## 0. The brief's hard constraint

No medical, injury-prevention or "tap early" advice (`docs/article-briefs.md` §0). The article contains none and says so in its first section. Where a rule book states a safety rationale (UWW Article 30, "The athletes' security shall prevail at all times"), the article reports that the document says it. One academy sheet tells its own students when to tap; the article reports that the sheet does so, does not quote that instruction, and does not adopt it.

## 3. Contradictions and how handled

- **The brief expected a coach's power to stop a match in the IBJJF rule book.** None was found. The word "coach" does not occur in the extracted text, and "professor" occurs only in a disciplinary clause outside the match rules. The article says we looked and did not find one, and reports what the UWW and ADCC texts do say about coaches. The only corner power found anywhere is the ABC's "Corner Stoppage", filed as a TKO.
- **IBJJF version label.** The download page calls the book "v6.0"; every page footer of the PDF reads "VERSION 6.1 2024". The source title records both.
- **Loss of consciousness.** IBJJF: its own form of decision (2.4.1). UWW: win "by submission". ABC: "Technical Submission". Printed as a difference, not reconciled.
- **UWW screaming.** Article 30 counts screaming as a verbal submission; Article 43 lists "Talking/screaming during the bout" as a caution-level illegal action. The article prints both and says the document does not reconcile them.
- **ABC edition.** The August 2026 header gives a "requested implementation date" of November 2027 for that year's rule changes. The passages used are unchanged from August 2025, so nothing in the article depends on which edition is in force. Recorded in `contestedNotes`.
- **Unmerged first-week draft** (`origin/content/journal-batch-4`, `how-long-to-blue-belt-and-other-first-week-questions`) says no published gym policy on how to tap had been found among the pages it read. This piece cites one (Impact Jiu Jitsu). The two are compatible as written, but if both merge, that sentence in the other draft could point here.

## 5. Claims cut, and why

- *Cut:* any statement that the tap is "the first thing a beginner is taught" or is taught in "most" rooms. Unsourced generalisation; the piece opens on the documents instead.
- *Cut:* the judo origin of the tap and the word *maitta*. The IJF's online rules returned no rule text to a direct fetch, and the only description found by search was a general-audience page. Not citable to the standard.
- *Cut:* what "Mate" means in the ADCC line. The page does not say; the article says it does not explain the words.
- *Cut:* any history of the tap in MMA, any named match, late-release incident or athlete.
- *Cut:* the tap-early instructions found on four academy pages. Reporting them at length would have turned description into advice. The article notes only that one sheet "goes on to tell its students when to tap".
- *Cut:* UWW's note that an illegal action used to prevent a submission brings immediate disqualification (verified, removed for length).
- *Cut:* any claim about what rooms generally do when someone taps. One sheet is evidence about one room.
- *Not stated:* that the UWW January 2021 edition is current. Not established.

## 6. Editorial notes

- The first section's statement that the piece gives no advice about when to tap is load-bearing; keep it.
- The last section's two "practical consequences" are about reading an event's rules and asking a room what its convention is. Neither is about a hold. If an editor wants them firmer, they must not become tapping advice.
- If the IBJJF publishes a new rule book, re-check 2.1.1 to 2.1.4 and the absence claims in §1 B3 before publication.
