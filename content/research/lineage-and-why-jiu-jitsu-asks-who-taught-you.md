## 1. What the piece is

A philosophy piece on lineage as an idea. It recites no individual's lineage, names no living person's rank, and makes no claim about any person's or school's legitimacy. Three things in it are this site's own and are labelled so in the copy and in `contestedNotes`: the description of the custom, the argument about what a lineage is for, and the point that a chain records promotions and not teaching.

## 5. Contradictions and oddities

1. **IBJJF article numbering.** The index page of S1 lists Article 1 Introduction, 2 Belts, 3 Minimum Ages, 4 Minimum Period, 5 Degree System, 6 Requirements for the certificate, 7 Professors and Instructors. The body prints Article 1 Belts through Article 6 Professor and Additional Professor and Article 7 Athletes with Provisional Graduation. Internal cross-references ("see Article 7" for provisional status, "Article 3.1.3", "Article 4.2.1 VI") follow the body. The article cites body numbers and says so in `contestedNotes`.
2. **Version marks.** The cover and back page say June 2026 and "VERSION 3.3"; page footers say "VERSION 3.1 2025". The article gives only the date.
3. **Maeda and rank.** Cairus (never awarded a black belt in Brazil) against Drysdale (five promoted to primeiro galao, meaning unclear). Reported exactly as the published Maeda article reports it; not resolved.
4. **Franca.** Drysdale against Borges, as in the Maeda article and the unmerged Fadda draft. This piece says less than either and decides nothing.
5. **Kodokan and the teacher's name.** The Kodokan is the central-body contrast, yet its own form asks for the applicant's sensei. Stated in the article instead of being smoothed over.

## 6. Claims cut, and why

- **The IBJJF "Belt History" step.** The membership checklist strings in the script include a step labelled Belt History ("Fill out the Belt History form and have your listed instructor sign the form."). The page text is assembled by script and it could not be established which applicants are shown the step, so the sentence was cut.
- **The tenth degree clause (4.1.5 X).** It says the tenth degree "was given only to the pioneers of Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu" and lists five first names. Cut for length, and because it adds named individuals to a piece that names as few as possible.
- **Article 7.4.1** (a second-degree professor may approve a provisional black belt who was a provisional brown belt under the same professor or academy). Left out as detail; the article says "at least three degrees (Article 7.4)", which is the general rule as printed.
- **Drysdale's 2019 article** (1938 newspaper listing Franca for the Gracie academy). The archived copy returned 429 today, so nothing from it is used.
- **Pedreira's statement that nobody qualified ever certified Carlos Gracie.** It is in the passage quoted, and it is a claim about a person's standing. Out of scope for a piece that makes no claim about anyone's legitimacy.
- **Pedreira's method ("built on newspaper archives").** The Maeda article says it; not re-verified today, so he is described only as the author of the Choque histories, which his own text cites.
- **"Peer-reviewed" for Cairus.** Not re-verified today; the article says "a journal article".
- **The UKBJJA's own history paragraph** (Maeda, Carlos Gracie, 1917). It repeats the popular version that the Maeda article shows to be contested. Not used.
- **Any statement about how common the custom is, or where it came from.** No source. The custom is described as this site's observation.
- **The Kodokan sentence beginning "It is important to note that the dan ranks of one's own country..."** Paraphrased, because the wording is on the banned-constructions list.
- **Kodokan examination fees and the list of 16 committee countries.** Irrelevant to the argument.
- **Any living person's rank, any individual's lineage, any comparison between lines.** By design.
