## 3. Contradictions and tensions

1. **Which joint the kimura attacks.** Kodokan defines ude-garami as a technique to "twist and lock the opponent's elbow joint". Kesting: "rotation of the arm relative to the shoulder". Library `kimura-is-a-grip-first`: rotates the shoulder through a bent elbow. The existing Journal kimura article already notes judo classes it with the locks on the elbow. The article reports all of these and does not settle it.
2. **"Following judo usage".** The Library's blood-choke entry says many coaches, "following judo usage", reserve strangle for the blood mechanic and choke for the airway one. The Kodokan's own English texts read today do not keep the words apart: every shime-waza definition says "choke", and the kata textbook uses both verbs. The article quotes the Library's sentence without the "following judo usage" clause and reports what the Kodokan prints. It does not say the Library is wrong (the Library's claim is about coaches' usage), but **the owner may want to look at that clause in the Library entry.**
3. **Hadaka-jime placement.** Kodokan defines hadaka-jime as "putting the right forearm on the opponent's throat from behind"; the kata text puts the wrist on the throat. The Library's rear naked strangle puts the crook of the elbow in front of the throat with pressure on the sides. The article does not describe the placement and makes no claim that the two are mechanically identical; it says only that hadaka-jime "is the hold our Library calls the rear naked strangle", which follows the existing Journal article.
4. **Where a neck crank belongs.** BJJ Mental Models: a type of choke. IBJJF table wording: "Choke with spinal lock" and "Spinal lock without choke", so a spinal lock is separate from a choke. Kodokan: no such technique among the 22. Reported as a difference.
5. **Torso compression.** Shime-waza (do-jime) to the Kodokan; "compression choke" to BJJ Mental Models; a "Lock" in the IBJJF row name. Reported as a difference.
6. **BJJ Mental Models internal inconsistency.** Breaking Mechanics: summary line says "four types of breaks", the next sentence says "three different types", and four are listed. The article follows the list; noted in `contestedNotes`.
7. **Kesting's "Air Chokes" has an apparent slip** ("chokes usually take longer than chokes"). Not quoted.

## 5. The IBJJF table

The table that follows 6.2.3 M has six division columns and numbered rows keyed to drawings. In pdftotext output the row names extract cleanly but the row numbers drift against the names and the per-column marks do not extract at all. The article therefore uses the row names as vocabulary and makes **no** statement about which division any technique is prohibited in. No cell of the table has been reconstructed. The row names were cross-checked against the federation's one-page illegal-moves sheet, where they read the same.

Absence claims ("submission hold" is not defined) rest on searching the extracted text; text inside images would not be found. Hedged in the body ("in the text we extracted") and in `contestedNotes`.

## 6. Claims cut, and why

- **"A beginner hears forty submission names in a first year."** A number with no source. The opening says "a long list".
- **"Nearly all submissions belong to the two families."** A proportion with no source. The piece says what the sorting is and where it leaks.
- **"The oldest sorting"** (first draft wording). Unsourced superlative; now "the simplest sorting", which is the site's characterisation.
- **Any statement of which divisions the IBJJF prohibits a hold in.** The table columns did not extract (§5).
- **IBJJF 6.2.3 clause I** (severe foul for strangling with the hands around the neck without the gi, or thumb pressure on the windpipe) and the observation that "strangles" appears once in the extracted text. Accurate, drafted, then cut for length. Clause J (blocking air to the nose or mouth with the hands) likewise unused.
- **Time for a strangle to take effect, unconsciousness, pain, deaths in police restraint, rib breakage, "cranks are not recommended in training", "a safer way of training".** All appear in the Grapplearts and BJJ Mental Models pages. All are medical or safety content and were left out.
- **"The majority of common chokes in Jiu-Jitsu are blood chokes"** (BJJ Mental Models). A frequency claim with no data behind it on the page. Not used.
- **Kesting's 2013 Facebook poll of "best" submissions** (triangle from guard, armbar, kimura). A ranking, from a self-selected poll. Not used.
- **Kesting's statement that the heel hook is "the most effective leglock"** and his account of what the IBJJF banned as of 2017. A ranking, and out of date. Only the names are used.
- **BJJ Mental Models' "3/3 joints" rule as a claim about what a submission requires.** The entry is cited only for its list of the three joints per limb; the closing section is built on the Library's "joint above fixed, joint below held", and the two formulations were not reconciled.
- **Who writes BJJ Mental Models.** The entries are unsigned on the page; no author is named in the article.
- **Kesting's rank** (the site banner calls him a fourth-degree black belt). Not needed and not independently verified.
- **Omoplata as a shoulder lock.** The name is in the IBJJF table but no source read today says what it attacks. Not mentioned.
- **A pointer to the Journal piece on pins.** Cut for length.
- **Catch wrestling's "double wristlock"** is kept, but only as something "our kimura article records"; it was not re-sourced today.
