## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **Within one paper.** Andreato et al. give high-intensity action duration as ~4 s (abstract) and ~3 s (discussion). Not reconciled; both printed, with the discrepancy named.
- **Folk claim versus measurement.** The community claim that sweat makes grips fail is contradicted in direction by the only friction measurement located, which found friction *rising* with hydration. Not reconciled and not resolved: the article states that the measurement is of a different system (skin sliding on textile at low load, not a hand closing on a limb) and that the grappling case is unmeasured. **No claim about sweat is made in either direction.**
- **Grip type.** The one study that varied grip configuration found the *cloth* grip weaker than a standard handle, which is the opposite of what a reader might assume from "gi grips are stronger". The article reports the finding as stated.

## 7. Image requirements

- A two-panel diagram: a closed cloth grip drawn as a latch, and a hand on a wrist drawn as a contact that requires continuous effort. Labelled key beneath.
- A conceptual chart of the critical-torque idea: a line, an area above it labelled as a finite reservoir, and a note that the reservoir does not refill while above the line. **It must be drawn without axis values**, because the study's values are for a laboratory handgrip device.
- **No photographs of grips on a training partner.** Skin contact imagery in a piece that mentions sweat invites exactly the hygiene reading the house rules forbid.

## 8. Rights concerns

- All four studies are cited, not reproduced. Two are open-access on PMC; the figures used are from abstracts and results text.
- No athlete, coach or brand is named.
- No garment or grip-training product is mentioned anywhere, deliberately. The temptation to bridge from grip to fabric is the one this piece exists to resist.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* "sweat makes no-gi grips slip." Unsourced, and the nearest measurement runs the other way. Replaced with an explicit statement that it is unmeasured.
- *Cut:* any figure for how long a no-gi grip lasts. No study measures it. The word "half-life" in the title is flagged inside the article as a way of thinking rather than a measured constant.
- *Cut:* a single percentage for the within-match handgrip decline. The paper reports pre/post means and SDs, not a percentage; the article gives a range derived from those means and says so.
- *Cut:* any claim that gi grips are stronger than no-gi grips. The one relevant study found the cloth grip produced *lower* force than a standard handle, in an isometric rowing task, which is not the same claim as either.
- *Cut:* any training prescription. Both the Andreato and *Sports* papers make training recommendations; the article reports the measurements and not the recommendations, because the house rules forbid turning study findings into advice.
- *Cut:* any garment claim about grip endurance, moisture management or friction. None is made, and no fabric is mentioned.
