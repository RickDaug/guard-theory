## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **The contextual interference effect itself.** Not reconciled, deliberately. The article prints Czyż et al.'s numbers, then names Ammar and Schöllhorn's published objection, then states that a coach told "the research is settled" has been told wrong *in either direction*. This is the article's central methodological position.
- **Lab vs applied within the same meta-analysis.** Both subgroup results are printed. The article does not resolve them, because the authors do not.
- **Deliberate practice.** Macnamara et al.'s estimate is itself contested — the definition and measurement of deliberate practice in that literature is disputed. Recorded in `contestedNotes` and the figure is used only as a bound, not as a claim.

## 8. Rights concerns

Low. All named individuals are researchers described in their professional capacity, with their published positions represented accurately and their disagreement described as a disagreement. No gym, coach or organisation is named or characterised.

One item to watch: the article reports that one group of researchers has publicly criticised another group's methodology. That is accurately sourced to the critics' own published comment and response article, and both are cited. It should not be paraphrased into anything stronger than "challenged" and "criticism" without re-reading both documents.

The SportRxiv item is a **preprint**. The article calls it a "formal comment", which is accurate, and pairs it with the peer-reviewed 2025 response so the position does not rest on the preprint alone. If a reviewer prefers, the preprint citation can be dropped and the peer-reviewed response carries the point on its own.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* the entire injury-aware-training topic, which was one of the brief's suggested options. Every version of it required a medical or injury-prevention claim, which the brief and `src/content/policies` both forbid.
- *Cut:* the age-subgroup results from Czyż et al. The retrieved values did not read coherently (a medium effect reported alongside a non-significant p-value), and rather than guess at the correct pairing the whole subgroup was dropped.
- *Cut:* the random-effects pooled estimate (0.71) alongside the three-level estimate (0.63). Printing both invites cherry-picking; the more conservative model is cited and this file records the other.
- *Cut:* any claim that positional sparring improves anything. There is no study. This was the most tempting cut to avoid making, and the article is structurally organised around not making it.
- *Cut:* any number for how a typical class divides its hour between the three activities. No source exists; §6's argument is made qualitatively instead.
- *Cut:* Shea & Morgan (1979) and the standard historical account of the contextual interference effect. Both are cited inside the meta-analysis but neither was read in full text here, and citing a foundational paper from a secondary description is exactly the habit this Journal declines.
- *Cut:* the "how a room's warm-up encodes its values" angle, also offered by the brief. It is a good subject and it could not be supported by anything except assertion; it is worth commissioning separately as an explicitly interpretive piece.
- *Cut:* any suggestion about how a reader should change their training. The closing paragraph deliberately ends on a coach, per the pattern established by the equipment and rulesets articles.

## 10. Editorial notes

- §2 ("Nobody has studied this") must stay where it is, before any evidence is introduced. Moving it later, or cutting it, converts a careful piece into an ordinary one that borrows authority without declaring the loan.
- §3 is the article's methodological centre: a live, named, public academic dispute reported as a dispute. If the field converges later, this section needs rewriting rather than deleting.
- The Maloney study is taekwondo, ten athletes, simulated competition. Every restatement of it in the article carries at least one of those qualifiers. Keep it that way.
- The closing paragraph carries both the no-medical-claims statement and the coach deferral. They are load-bearing; do not compress them into one sentence.
- If the Journal later publishes anything on class structure, warm-ups or curriculum, it must be consistent with §6's position that these choices are legible and mostly inherited rather than chosen — or explain why it differs.
