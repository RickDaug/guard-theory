### This site's argument (labelled in copy and in contestedNotes)

- Reading the route as a statement of what a pass has to beat; "feet first, then knees, then arms".
- Around has to beat the turn of the hips; over has to put a knee or shin down; under lifts the legs and puts the feet and knees above the passer; through arrives between the knees where arms and the underhook matter.
- Filing the knee slide under over, and the leg drag under around with the legs doing the travelling.
- That both rule books leave the route open, so a sort by route has no official list behind it.
- Reading the list from underneath as a retention checklist; an around pass as an attack on the retention piece's angle variable.
- The closing two questions.

## 3. Contradictions and wrinkles

- **The count.** The only source that states a count of routes gives three: over, under, around. "Through" is not in it. It appears in Kesting's knee cut phrase and in the Ruiz rule, which contrasts around with "through the center" and has no over or under. No source read counts four, and none says whether through is a fourth route or a kind of over. The working headline ("Around, over or through") dropped under, which the one stated count includes, and promoted through, which it does not; the headline was changed to the sourced three and through has its own section.
- **Same author, different "fundamentally".** Kesting with Kang: "Fundamentally there are 3 kinds of guard passes" (route). Kesting 2019: "Fundamentally there are two styles of guard passing" (speed and pressure). These are two axes, and the article says so.
- **Library families against Kesting's styles.** The Library has three families (pressure, movement, leg drag); Kesting has two styles (speed, pressure). The article reports both and treats the Library as the reference for that axis.
- **Knee line, two uses.** BJJ Mental Models: the line between your own knees, extended to a triangle with the groin; in passing, the passer's knee must get out of the opponent's triangle. Library: the line across the bottom player's knees, "past the knee line is what passed means". Recorded in contestedNotes.
- **Where the knee slide belongs.** Kang and Kesting's article says passers in its setting mostly go over or around, and the knee slide is one of three passes pictured with it, but no caption assigns it a route. Kesting elsewhere says the knee cut knifes through the legs; the Library says it cuts through the space between them. The article files it under over as an inference and says so.
- **IBJJF end position.** 4.2 requires side control or north-south. Kesting's old school toreando "typically" ends in knee on belly. Not taken up in the copy.

## 5. Claims cut, and why

- BJJ Mental Models' *Phases of Passing* ("disentangle, control, pass") as a fourth axis: sourced and matched, cut for length. The source was removed with it.
- Kesting's note that the over-under was created by combining the stack pass and the toreando: it is his summary of what Fabio Gurgel says in a video, not his own written claim, and was cut with the paragraph on passes changing route.
- That Kesting repeated the three-stage sentence in *The Unpassable Guard*: true, cut for length.
- The tripod position ("one of your legs between his thighs") as a third instance of through: the source sentence has a typo and describes a position, not a pass.
- "In NHB you most often pass over or around the legs": a claim about a setting the piece does not take up.
- Any statement of which route is more common, higher percentage, or better for a body type or age. Kesting's remarks on age, size and athleticism were read and not used.
- "The Toreando is often one of the first guard passes that starts to work for beginners": an instructor's impression, not needed.
- History claims: Geesink's 1977 book, who popularised the leg drag, who first used tripod passing.
- IBJJF 3.4 (pass followed by mount adds to seven points) and 5.6.2 (advantages): read, not needed.
