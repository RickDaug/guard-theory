## 3. Contradictions and gaps — how each was handled

- **"Ebi".** Kesting states that the shrimp came from judo, "where it is known as 'ebi'". Neither Kodokan document read today uses the word, and neither describes solo exercises at all (one is a kata textbook, the other a list of technique definitions). That is an absence in two documents, not a contradiction, and the article does not present it as one. The body attributes the statement to Kesting by name; `contestedNotes` records that the Kodokan documents read do not use the term. The article does **not** say the Kodokan teaches or names the drill.
- **What "upa" means.** Kesting uses it for the bridge-and-roll escape and, in the same article, for the bridged position. The article says so and defines its own two terms.
- **Bridge first, or frames first.** Kesting's side control article says "Bridge, then Shrimp" and puts blocking the crossface before either; the Library's side control entry says frames, bridge, shrimp, knee; the Library's mount entry says bridge, hip escape, elbow, knee. These agree on bridge-before-hip-escape, which is the only ordering the article asserts. The frame's position in the sequence is given per position, following the Library.
- **Dating the kata sentence.** The textbook dates the kata to "around 1885 to 1886" but the text is the form "Amended on February 1st, 2006". The article states both facts and explicitly declines to date the quoted escape example.
- **The textbook's own slips.** Under kami-shiho-gatame the English text has "turning Uke in the opposite direction" and "inserting his knees or feet against Uke", where the sense requires Tori. Neither phrase is quoted; A12 paraphrases as "the knees or feet, put between the two bodies".

## 6. Claims cut, and why

- **"Every beginner is shown these in a first warm-up."** The brief's framing. No source; replaced with what Kesting actually writes (A1 to A3).
- **Anything about bridging onto the head or the neck.** Kesting's bridge article discusses it, in injury terms. Cut entirely under the no-safety-advice rule.
- **Bridging onto the balls of the feet for height.** The subject of Kesting's bridge article and attributed there to watching a named competitor. It is a technique tip, not part of the argument, and was left out.
- **How a bridge-and-roll from mount is scored.** The IBJJF sweep article (4.6.1 to 4.6.3) describes sweeps as starting "with the opponent in his/her guard or half-guard" and then says its examples "are merely illustrative". A paragraph inferring that a reversal from under mount scores nothing was drafted in outline and dropped: the rule book does not say so in terms, and the article would have been stating a referee's decision the text does not state.
- **Wrestling's use of the bridge and why it differs.** Kesting mentions it in one line. No wrestling rule book was fetched, so nothing is said.
- **"Most people only know one or two ways to shrimp."** Kesting's line, repeated in two of his articles. It is an unmeasured generalisation about people and was not repeated.
- **Any claim that the Kodokan teaches ebi as a solo drill.** Not found in anything read. See section 3.
- **Any claim that solo drilling does or does not transfer.** No study located. The article says so.
- **Tate-shiho-gatame as "the mount".** The Kodokan definition was read, but the equivalence would have been ours, the kata does not include that hold, and the piece did not need it.

## 7. Editorial notes

- The Kodokan paragraphs are doing a narrow job: showing that an institutional text pairs the arch with the knee and the hip twist with the knee. They are not evidence that either works, and `contestedNotes` says so. Do not strengthen them.
- The "What follows is ours" sentence at the top of the warm-up section must stay where it is. Everything after it in that section, up to the paragraph on Kesting's drill list, is argument.
- The closing paragraph carries the coach deferral in the pattern of `why-the-underhook-decides-half-guard`.
