## 0. Why the number changed from five to six

The brief proposed five ties (collar tie, underhook, overhook, wrist control, two-on-one) and allowed the number to follow the sources. The only sentence found that lists the no-gi replacements for cloth grips is Kesting's, in *5 Tips for Switching from Gi to No Gi*:

> Without the gi you'll have to rely much more on wrestling-based controls like wrist grips, collar ties, underhooks, overhooks, two-on-ones, and bearhugs.

That is the brief's five plus the bearhug. The article follows the sentence, says the list is illustrative ("like"), says where the list stops is this site's decision, and records in the bearhug section that five was the plan. Other counts found: USA Wrestling's coaches' test refers to 8 contact (tie-up) positions (freestyle syllabus) and 6 tie-ups (Greco-Roman syllabus); the Dick's Pro Tips guide gives three types. All three are reported in the first section.

## 3. Contradictions and gaps

- **How many ties.** Kesting six (as examples), USA Wrestling eight and six (by syllabus), Dick's guide three (by type). The article prints all of them and says its own number is a choice.
- **Which eight, which six.** The USA Wrestling page is a test without answers. Question 34's four options each list six tie-ups and differ from one another; the article does not say which is correct and does not use any option as a fact.
- **Two-on-one or Russian tie.** Witulski (USA Wrestling, 2002) uses both for one tie. Malinconico (FloWrestling, 2020) says it is a two-on-one and not a Russian tie. His reasons are behind a paywall. The article reports the disagreement, uses two-on-one, and gives no origin for "Russian".
- **Collar tie or head tie.** Kesting and the Dick's guide write collar tie; Witulski writes head-tie and head tie. The article treats them as one tie. No source read says so in a single sentence; the equation rests on the two descriptions matching (hand at the back of the neck / on the head, used for the snapdown in both). Flagged for the owner in section 7.
- **Overhook or whizzer.** Kesting gives them as the same thing ("aka whizzer") and the Library entry agrees. Some writing found by search distinguishes a whizzer as an overhook applied with downward pressure; none of that was from a citable source and the article does not go into it.
- **Bearhug or bodylock.** Not defined in anything read. See B2.
- **Kesting on slipperiness.** The sentence before A29 says "Because everything is slipperier you may not be able to maintain your grips for as long". The grip decay article cut the sweat claim as unsourced, so this half of Kesting's sentence is not quoted or relied on.

## 6. Claims cut, and why

- **"Borrowed mostly from wrestling" as a historical claim.** The brief's framing. The article says only that Kesting calls them "wrestling-based controls". No history of how the terms reached jiu-jitsu was sourced.
- **Where "Russian tie" and "whizzer" come from.** Nothing citable found. Not discussed.
- **"Over-under" as the name for one underhook each.** The USA Wrestling test uses the term but does not define it on the page. Cut.
- **A definition of pummelling from USA Wrestling.** The test offers four candidate definitions as multiple-choice options and no answer. Not used.
- **Varieties of two-on-one (inside, outside, under-grab, over-grab).** They appear only as test options. Not used.
- **Any claim about which tie is strongest, most common in jiu-jitsu, or learned first.** No source. The one frequency statement kept is Witulski's about the head-tie, attributed and flagged.
- **"The collar tie controls posture."** Drafted; the sources say it controls the head and is used to break stance. The sentence now stops at the head.
- **Anything about strikes.** Kesting's glossary mentions wrist and elbow control and the bearhug in MMA. Out of scope.
- **Kani basami and the Viktor roll**, which sit in the same Kesting article as the collar tie description, with his injury remarks. Not mentioned; no safety content of any kind is in the piece.
- **"Because everything is slipperier".** See section 3.
- **Kesting's kimura-versus-two-on-one distinction beyond his one sentence.** The Library entry is pointed to instead.
- **IBJJF or ADCC scoring.** Not relevant to names; the half guard note already covers it.
