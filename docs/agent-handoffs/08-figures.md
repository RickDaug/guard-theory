## Image licensing — what was accepted and why

Only two licence families were accepted: public domain, and Creative Commons stated explicitly on a Wikimedia Commons file page. Every file page was read as raw wikitext so the licence **template** was inspected rather than a rendered summary, and every image was downloaded and **looked at** before being kept.

**Accepted:**

- **Maeda** — `{{PD-Japan}}`. Studio portrait from a Japanese book published by Shimazu Shobo; subject died 1941.
- **Carlos Gracie / Helio Gracie** — `{{Arquivo Nacional PD-license}}`. Both from the Fundo Correio da Manha at Brazil's national archive, uploaded under the Wikimedia Brazil GLAM partnership, with accession numbers recorded in the credit lines. Photographers recorded as unknown. Both are TIFFs; the JPEG renderings generated by Commons were downloaded.
- **Rickson Gracie / Marcelo Garcia** — `{{PD-user-w}}` and `{{PD-user}}`, i.e. the photographer released the work into the public domain.
- **Royce Gracie** — `{{self|cc-by-4.0}}` by Shane Balkowitsch, a 2025 wet-plate collodion portrait. Attribution is in the credit line as CC BY requires. Best portrait in the set.
- **Roger Gracie** — `{{self|cc-by-sa-3.0|GFDL}}`. File page names both an uploader and a photographer; the credit line names both.
- **Kyra Gracie** — `{{GFDL|migration=relicense}}` (effective CC BY-SA 3.0) with a Wikimedia VRT permission ticket. **No photographer is named on the file page**; the credit line says exactly that rather than inventing one.

**Rejected:**

- **Oswaldo Fadda** — the only Commons candidate is a *painted portrait* uploaded as "own work" in 2014, nine years after his death, whose file page does not identify the sitter. Licence unusable, identification unverified.
- **Carlson Gracie** — the only Commons candidate claims "own work" in 2023 for a photograph of a man who died in 2006. `Category:Carlson Gracie` is empty.
- **Ffion Davies** — a candidate exists under YouTube's CC BY licence with a Commons licence review, but it is a still from an OnlyFans-branded promotional video. Legally arguable, editorially wrong for this index. She was dropped from the shortlist for other reasons too (see below).
- **Andre Galvao** — a correctly licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 photograph with a VRT ticket exists, but it is a watermarked action shot in which the subject's face is largely hidden. He was dropped in favour of Rickson Gracie.

## Claims cut

Every cut is recorded in the relevant research file. The ones that matter:

1. **Royce Gracie's 2007 nandrolone positive.** One community-reference source states it. A corroborating *New York Times* report exists but **nytimes.com is not retrievable from this environment** (403 to curl, refused by WebFetch), and no other accessible primary or established-news source was found. A doping allegation about a living person on one community source is not publishable here. Cut from the body; the cut itself is disclosed in `contestedNotes`. Reinstate with a primary commission record, and include his response.
2. **Carlson Gracie's birth and death dates.** His individual BJJ Heroes fighter page returns a Cloudflare challenge; the only place his full dates were seen was a Wikipedia list, which is not a source here. `lifespan` is left unset and only the death *year* appears, sourced from the team-history page.
3. **Rickson Gracie's "400-0".** Reported as a myth with the contradicting sambo loss, never as a record. His Parkinson's diagnosis, though public and sourced, was cut as a health matter irrelevant to the contribution.
4. **All "first woman" claims about Kyra Gracie.** Women competed and won before her; no source supports a first.
5. **All invention claims** for Marcelo Garcia (arm drag, butterfly guard, X-guard, north-south choke all predate him) and for Carlos Gracie (no source documents a technique he originated).
6. **Roger Gracie's total title count.** His own site says ten; BJJ Heroes refers to a fourteenth. Same career, different arithmetic. No total is asserted.
7. **Ranking language throughout.** "Most successful academy in history", "greatest judoka to ever live", "one of the best of his generation" and similar all appear in the sources and none appear here.

## Risks and known weaknesses

- **BJJ Heroes carries too much of the load.** It is the only source with biographical depth on most modern competitors, and it reproduces itself across pages. Where it is the only source, the entry says so. It also contradicts itself in ways this set now documents: the Rio federation is 1967 on one page and 1969 on another; Rickson is "18 years old" in a fight that took place when he was 21.
- **No official ADCC results archive was reachable today.** `adcc.tv` and `adccsubmission.com` failed DNS; `adcombat.com` sits behind a Cloudflare challenge. Every ADCC title in this set therefore rests on BJJ Heroes and is flagged. The site *was* reachable on 2026-08-03 for an earlier Journal piece, so retry before publication and upgrade those citations.
- **graciemag.com serves injected spam links** in its page furniture. The editorial content on the Fadda page reads as genuine and is corroborated on the 1967 federation, but the 1955 *Revista dos Esportes* quotation from Helio Gracie rests on GracieMag alone.
- **`bjjheroes.com` intermittently returns HTTP 403** to automated requests. All cited pages returned full content today, some on a retry. They are live links, not dead ones — do not remove them if a link checker flags them without a manual check.
- **Six of the ten profiles are Gracies.** That reflects who the documented record covers rather than an editorial judgement, and it is exactly why Oswaldo Fadda is in the set despite having no usable portrait. If the index grows, the obvious additions that would improve the balance are Luiz Franca, Rolls Gracie, Ricardo de la Riva (already the subject of a Journal article), Xande Ribeiro (CC0 portrait available on Commons), Andre Galvao and Marcus Almeida.
- **One woman in ten.** Stated plainly rather than papered over. Adding Leticia Ribeiro, Hannette Staack or Beatriz Mesquita — all IBJJF Hall of Fame inductees — would be the substantive fix, though portrait licensing was not checked for any of them.
- **Two portraits are small.** Rickson at 244×295 and Roger at 307×533 are the largest correctly licensed images that exist on Commons for those subjects. They will not survive aggressive upscaling in a hero slot; design around them.
- **Kyra Gracie's portrait is a publicity photograph** with sponsor branding, not reportage. It is properly licensed. The alt text describes it honestly.
