## 3. Contradictions

- **C1. Year of first publication.** The SFPL catalogue record for the first edition gives [1970]. Shambhala's 2020 page speaks of "the fifty years since its original publication", which agrees. The Zen Center's books page, in its blurb for Branching Streams Flow in the Darkness, says Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind "was published in 1972". The article uses 1970 on the catalogue record and records the 1972 figure in contestedNotes.
- **C2. The sentence itself.** The publisher's quotation ("In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, but in the expert's there are few.") and the transcript ("In beginner's mind we have many possibilities, but in expert mind there is not much possibilities.") differ. This is not a conflict; the article prints both and says which is which. That the difference is editing is an inference from the catalogue's "Edited by Trudy Dixon" and the Zen Center's "edited talks"; the body says only that the publisher's line is tidier.
- **C3. Attention or modesty.** The article argues the phrase is about attention first. Against that: the transcript's passage on not saying "I know what is Zen"; the modern sense Tsuchiya reports (not forgetting one's initial resolve); and the dictionary gloss noted in section 5. The body concedes the transcript passage and contestedNotes says a reader could weigh it differently.
- **C4. Does expertise narrow?** S8 summarises an earlier result as experts being pulled down about three standard deviations by a familiar solution. S9 found novices and experts equally likely to choose the familiar move on its one critical problem, and S8 itself says resistance rises with skill. The article reports all three and builds its sixth section on the tension.

## 5. Claims cut, and why

- **That the phrase hangs on gym walls, or is common in jiu-jitsu.** No count exists. One academy post is cited as one post.
- **A second academy example** (Princeton BJJ, "Always the Beginner": "no expectations, no hubris"). Cut because the page is posted under one person's name and written in another's voice, and the attribution could not be settled from the page.
- **A dictionary definition of shoshin.** Jisho.org (built on the JMdict project, which accepts user submissions) gives "one's original intention; one's initial enthusiasm" and "inexperience; naivety; greenness", and glosses the proverb as, among other things, "never lose your humility". Not cited because of the open-submission source. It is relevant to C3 and is the strongest thing found for the humility reading. The Soto Zen glossary page was fetched and has no entry for the word.
- **That Zeami's saying uses the word shoshin, its date, or the treatise it comes from.** S5 gives the saying only in English and names The Flower Mirror; the romanised wording, a 1424 date and the title Kakyo appeared only in search-result summaries and on pages not read. Cut.
- **That Suzuki attributed the idea to Dogen.** The transcript says "The founder of our school emphasized this point" and names nobody. Cut.
- **Where the talk was given.** Not on the page read. Cut.
- **Anything about the book's contents beyond the publisher's blurb**, including the 1970 edition's preface, later sales figures ("more than one million copies" is on a Zen Center page; not needed) and the "100 Best Spiritual Books" line.
- **The Keynes epigraph in S8.** A quotation inside a source, not checked against Keynes.
- **Luchins's water-jug experiment (1942).** Described in both papers; not read; not needed.
- **The radiology "satisfaction of search" parallel and the remarks on scientists and political experts** in the S9 discussion and S8 abstract. Speculative in the papers, and too near to medical territory.
- **First-fixation times** (407 ms experts, 719 ms novices) and the dwell measures in S9. Accurate, but they need more explanation than the piece has room for.
- **S8 Experiment 2.** Not read closely enough to report.
- **Expert-novice perception studies in sport.** None was fetched; the piece confines itself to the two chess papers and says nothing about sport perception research.
- **Any statement that an experienced grappler sees fewer options, or that a white belt sees more.** No source. The final section says the opposite: nothing here is evidence about jiu-jitsu.
