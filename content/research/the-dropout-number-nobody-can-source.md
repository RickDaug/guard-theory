## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

The whole article is a catalogue of contradictions, so nothing is reconciled. Every figure is printed with its owner and with whatever that owner says about its provenance. The article states explicitly that Guard Theory endorses none of them.

One structural contradiction is named in the text: figures for quitting *before blue belt* and figures for quitting *before black belt* are different quantities and get exchanged for each other in circulation.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following claims cut:**

- *Cut:* every dropout percentage as a statement of fact. The article endorses none.
- *Cut:* "ninety per cent" as the canonical version. It appears in circulation (Jiu Jitsu Haus) but no source establishes it as the original or dominant figure, and the standfirst lists it alongside the others rather than privileging it.
- *Cut:* any claim that injuries cause dropout. The injury study is used only as a methodological comparison; its injury rates are deliberately **not** quoted, to keep the article clear of injury claims entirely.
- *Cut:* a paragraph tracing the figure to an origin. No origin could be established for any version, and asserting one would have repeated the error the article is about.
- *Cut:* any statement about how many people train jiu-jitsu worldwide. The two totals encountered (5 million, 3 million) are unsourced and contradict each other; both are reported only as what those pages say.
- *Cut:* the claim that a typical black belt takes about ten years. BJJ Analytics states "10+ years" with no method; the article says only that a decade is *consistent with* the minimums, which is a much weaker and defensible statement.
- *Cut:* the SAGE URL for Crane & Temple (HTTP 403 to automated requests); ERIC used instead.

## 10. Editorial notes

- The ordering is deliberate: circulation first, provenance second, requirements third. Rewriting it to lead with "here is what we do know" would turn a piece about evidence into a piece about belts.
- The closing section must not acquire a number. Every draft of a piece like this wants to end by offering a better estimate, and there is no basis for one.
- If BJJ Analytics ever publishes a methodology, this article needs revisiting rather than deleting — the point about denominators survives regardless.
