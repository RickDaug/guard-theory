## 0. How the sources were read

Everything was fetched on 2026-11-11 with `curl` and a browser user agent, and read as raw HTML, as `pdftotext` output (the Library of Congress table) or as the Internet Archive's full-text file for a scanned book. Where a claim rests on a scanned book, the OCR text was used to find the passage and **the page image was then opened and read** for the passages that carry quotation marks or a spelling claim (marked "scan read" below). OCR alone was not trusted for spellings: it renders Hepburn's long u as "C" ("JCJUTSU"), italics as "qiu-qitsu", and the 1888 date as "2888".

## 3. Contradictory accounts and how each was handled

- **Where the i in "jitsu" comes from.** Dictionary.com derives the English word from a Japanese "jūjitsu, earlier jūjutsu"; AHD and Michaelis derive it from jūjutsu and do not explain the vowel. Hepburn 1888 prints both forms in one book. Not reconciled: the article prints all three positions and says that the popular explanations (mishearing, dialect) are not documented in anything read.
- **Date of "judo".** The IJF dates Kano's naming to May 1882; Dictionary.com dates the English word judo to 1885-90. These are different events (the naming, and the English borrowing) and only the first is used in the body.
- **"Jiudo" and "Judo" in 1888.** The Lindsay and Kano paper uses both on one page. Reported as it stands.
- **Brazilian spelling.** Michaelis prints an acute accent (jiu-jítsu); the IBJJF and CBJJ do not. Reported, not resolved.
- **JJIF's remark** that the designation Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu "was never actually used in Brazil itself" is unsourced on its page and was left out.

The art of wrestling, or of throwing others by sleight", which would show the pre-1886 spelling of the second half, but the entry could not be found on the page images checked (leaves n178, n179), so it is not used.
- *Cut:* "foreigners misheard jutsu" and "jitsu is a dialect form" as explanations. Nothing read documents either.
- *Cut:* the observation that "jitsu" is a different Japanese word with another meaning. No source read today states it.
- *Cut:* any year for the Miyake and Tani or Uyenishi books.
- *Cut:* JJIF's claim that "Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu" was never used in Brazil.
- *Cut:* Maeda's naturalisation and later dan grades, which Cairus mentions; they belong to the Maeda piece, which deliberately left them out.
- *Cut:* any claim about who first wrote the word in English, or which spelling is "correct".

## 10. Consistency with other Journal pieces

- **maeda-and-the-arrival-of-judo-in-brazil.** This piece repeats only two things from it: that Maeda reached Brazil in 1914, and Cairus's point that Maeda used the generic label jiu-jitsu. It adds the 1935 manual title and Maeda's closing remark from the same Cairus article, neither of which the Maeda piece uses. It does not retell the arrival, the fight record or the question of who taught Carlos Gracie.
- **where-the-guard-came-from (unmerged, batch 5).** No overlap in subject. That draft could not read the Kodokan's English pages; this one could (see the Kodokan note in section 2), which the integrator may want to pass on.
- The identification of the paper's co-author "J. Kano" with the founder of judo rests on the paper's own third-person description of Mr. J. Kano's school and is flagged in `contestedNotes`.
