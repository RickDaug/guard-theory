## 1. Claims made in the entry

### Treated as agreed

| # | Claim | Support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Born 1878, Funazawa village, Aomori Prefecture. | NDL column. |
| A2 | Enrolled at the Kodokan the year after first meeting judo (1897); 3rd dan 1901; one of the "Three Pillars of the Kodokan". | NDL column. |
| A3 | Left for the United States in 1907 with Tsunejiro Tomita. | NDL column ("In 1907, he departed for the United States with Tsunejiro Tomita"). Cairus places the departure during the Russo-Japanese War, which is a looser dating; the NDL year is used. |
| A4 | Arrived at the port of Santos in 1914; settled in Belem in 1915. | NDL column, verbatim: "he arrived at the port of Santos in 1914." |
| A5 | Died November 1941, aged 63, in Belem. | NDL column. |
| A6 | Performed in Mexico, Cuba and Peru on a professional wrestling circuit; the Belem troupe offered 5,000 francs at the Bar Paraense in 1915. | Cairus, sections on Mexico/Cuba/Peru and on the Folha do Norte announcement of October 1915. |
| A7 | A dojo was established at the Teatro Moderno in Belem in 1916 and Carlos Gracie began learning there. | Cairus: "Carlos Gracie, therefore, began to learn jiu-jitsu at the dojo which had been established at the Teatro Moderno sometime in 1916." |
| A8 | The apprenticeship "lasted no more than three years", under Maeda and his senior local student Jacyntho Ferro. | Cairus, verbatim. |
| A9 | Maeda ignored the Kodokan belt ranking system and taught an eclectic program mixing judo with wrestling. | Cairus. |
| A10 | From 1923 he was involved in settling Japanese immigrants in the Amazon; NDL describes him as guide and auditor for emigration projects. | Cairus (1923 onward) and NDL. |

### Treated as contested (stated as contested in the body **and** in `contestedNotes`)

| # | Claim | Positions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Arrival date/place. | NDL: Santos, 1914. Cairus: 1914 via Argentina/Uruguay. Drysdale: 25 September 1914, Sao Paulo, explicitly rejecting the circulated 14 November 1914 Porto Alegre date. |
| B2 | The 1,000-fights-unbeaten claim. | NDL repeats it; Pedreira: verifiable legitimate contests "could be counted on three or four hands and even some of those are questionable". |
| B3 | Whether Carlos Gracie was taught by Maeda personally. | Cairus: under Maeda *and* Ferro. Drysdale: Ferro the likely source. Pedreira: "None of them met him, with the possible but by no means certain exception of Carlos." |
| B4 | Whether Maeda was Kano's student / emissary. | Drysdale: "he was never a student of Jigoro Kano." Not asserted here. |
| B5 | Whether what he taught was "judo" or "jiu-jitsu". | Pedreira: Kodokan judo. Entry states this rather than using the terms interchangeably. |

## 4. Claims cut

- *Cut:* that Kano Jigoro sent Maeda abroad to spread judo. Contradicted by Drysdale.
- *Cut:* any total for his career contests. Pedreira's "three or four hands" is itself an estimate.
- *Cut:* the 14 November 1914 Porto Alegre landing. Explicitly rejected by Drysdale.
- *Cut:* his naturalisation, posthumous dan grade and the Tome-Acu settlement date — traceable only to Wikipedia, which is not a source here.
- *Cut:* the fourth-dan rank in the photo caption as a statement of fact; it is reported as a caption claim in `contestedNotes` only.
