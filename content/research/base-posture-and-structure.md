## 3. Contradictions and how each was handled

- **No text in Biernacki's own words.** The brief asked for his framework "in his own published words" if findable. It was not: his material is sold as video (the Grapplearts article embeds one; bjjconcepts.net is a video academy). The article says so in its second section and reports the definitions as Kesting's and BJJ Mental Models' wording. The only sentence attributed to Biernacki on any page read is a pull-quote in S1 about concept-based learning, which is off-topic and unused.
- **Kesting vs BJJ Mental Models on wording.** Both sets of definitions are printed side by side; neither is preferred.
- **Posture: spine (S1) vs arm and body position (S2), same author.** Printed as a difference in usage, with "Neither usage is wrong".
- **Posture in the Kodokan text is a stance.** Printed as a third usage. The article does not translate Jigo-tai or Shizen-hontai, because the textbook does not.
- **Straight-arm frames.** S1 favours the straight arm (with its own caveat); S5 and our `frames-versus-blocks` entry prefer fewer collapsible joints and a bent, supported elbow. Printed as a disagreement about arm shape under an agreed principle. The article sides with the Library and says it is doing so.
- **Kuzushi: balance (S7) vs all of alignment (S4).** Both printed; the site's preference is labelled.
- **Base: Library ("arrangement of the contact points on the floor") vs S1/S3 (force platform).** Reconciled in the working definition as supports plus the directions they cover, with the note that a support is something you push from as well as rest on. This extends the Library's sentence and does not contradict it.

## 9. Claims cut, and why

- *Cut:* Kesting's sentences linking bad posture to getting injured, and BJJ Mental Models' "hyperextend the joint and cause injury". Both are injury claims; the standing prohibitions forbid turning them into advice and the argument does not need them.
- *Cut:* Kesting's "live toes / dead toes" detail on kneeling base. Accurate to the source but a how-to detail that belongs in a Library entry, and it pushed the piece over length.
- *Cut:* glosses of Tsukuri and Kake, and translations of Jigo-tai and Shizen-hontai. The Kodokan textbook does not define them and no other institutional source was read. Writing the familiar translations from memory is exactly what this Journal does not do.
- *Cut:* any statement of when Biernacki devised the framework. Only the Grapplearts publication metadata is dated.
- *Cut:* the Davidovits centre-of-gravity figure (secondary quotation).
- *Cut:* BJJ Mental Models' closed guard scorecard example. It awards the bottom player "base" through shoulders and foot placement, which is an interesting extension of base to a supine player, but explaining it needed more room than the piece had.
- *Cut:* "base out", "posting" and other room slang for which no published source was found.
- *Softened:* "Real exchanges attack two of the three at once" became "often attack"; it is in the section labelled as our reading.
