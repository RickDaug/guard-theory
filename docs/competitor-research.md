# Guard Theory — Market & Competitor Research

**Category:** modern Brazilian jiu-jitsu / no-gi grappling apparel
**Purpose:** positioning and differentiation only
**All observations dated 2026-08-03 unless stated otherwise.**

---

## 0. How to read this document

**Evidence standard.** Every price, date and product claim carries the URL it came from and the date observed. Claims a brand makes about itself (founding dates, origin stories, "we were first") are labelled as self-reported. Anything that could not be established says **unverified** — there are no estimates here for revenue, headcount, ownership or founding dates.

**Currency.** Prices are given in the currency the storefront displayed. Several brands run multiple regional storefronts at different prices; both are given where observed.

**What is deliberately absent.** No taglines, product copy, image URLs or layout descriptions have been reproduced. Visual and verbal signatures are described in our own words. Nothing here should be used as a design template.

**Known research constraints — stated up front rather than buried:**
1. **Reddit and Instagram were inaccessible** (domain-level crawler blocks / login walls). There is therefore **no verified community-sentiment data** in this document. Follower counts, where mentioned, are search-snippet-sourced and unverified.
2. **Images were not viewed.** Visual signatures are inferred from product and colourway naming, site typography and CSS tokens, copy register, and textual descriptions — not from looking at photography. Photography style is flagged as unobserved where it matters.
3. **Several storefronts rate-limited repeated requests.** Where a brand's own site could not be read, public product feeds (`products.json`), retailer listings or search snippets were used instead, and the weaker evidence is flagged inline. **Tatami's own price pages never loaded** — its prices are the weakest evidence in this document.

### Shoyoroll

**Positioning.** A Southern California kimono house run like a streetwear label: small numbered batches on a fixed release calendar, priced at the top of the market, never discounted, with a gated inner tier.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **Spec precision and sizing depth.** The 2026 Tonal Competitor is stated as a 450 gsm pearl-weave jacket on 10 oz canvas pants, and a single kimono release carries roughly 48 variants — sizes 0–5 crossed with three separate fit blocks. Almost nobody cuts three body-shape blocks per size. ([2026 Tonal Competitor](https://www.shoyoroll.com/products/2026-tonal-competitor-kimono))
- **Sizing honesty.** Their FAQ publishes expected shrinkage by colour and tells customers to size down for a fitted result. ([FAQ](https://shoyoroll.com/pages/faq))
- **Price discipline.** 0 of 104 live products priced below compare-at. Nothing is ever on sale. (`shoyoroll.com/products.json`)
- **No-gi is engineered, not merch.** Internal pattern codes (TVT001.3–TVT001.8); a Made-in-USA line specifying four-way stretch and tapered fit. ([Atlas shorts](https://www.shoyoroll.com/products/atlas-training-fitted-shorts-reserve))

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** Essentially monochrome — the site's own accent token is a near-black on white, and red exists only as a sale-price colour that never fires. Type is plain grotesque with a monospace secondary, so the site reads like a spec sheet rather than a fashion site. Motifs borrow from American collegiate athletics: varsity pennants, script initials, stamp marks, university wrestling references. Copy is short and declarative — design intent and garment geometry, then a hard cut to materials and a ship window. No exclamation, no hype adjectives. Register: clinical confidence.

**Price band (USD).** SS rash guard $65 ($67 at 2XL); LS $75/$77; premium training rash guard $95/$97; shorts $70/$72, premium $90/$92; spats $85/$87; sports bra $45; tees $45–49, collab $60; hoody $90. Gi: Comp Standard $185–195, Competitor $250–260, collab $270–305.

**Release model.** Drops, not replenishment. **58 of 104 live products published on a Friday**, clustering fortnightly through 2026. Several 2026 Competitor kimonos show **0 of 32 variants available**. `/collections/reserve-store` and `/collections/be-first` require customer login; Reserve-tagged items price *below* the open equivalent (Atlas Reserve shorts $47 vs $70). Made-to-order pre-orders carry a 10% late-cancellation fee.

**Editorial.** A real archive, mostly dormant. `/blogs/syr` holds ~30 posts including a **TECHNICA technique series** (~20 entries, 2021–2022). **Critical caveat: nearly every TECHNICA entry has a body of ~3 characters — they are video embeds with no written article.** Last blog post 2025-02-28. Five genuinely written gi-education pieces all posted 2025-05-06, SEO-shaped.
**The serious output is not a blog.** The [Universal Grappling System](https://shoyoroll.com/pages/universal-grappling-system) is a constraint-based teaching curriculum aimed at instructors, with a stated pedagogy (no long warm-ups, no dead drilling, intentional sparring, reduced instructor burnout), sold in limited cycles — **CYCLE 2 shown as sold out** — with dedicated seminar pages for London, Paris and Milan. Pricing is not shown on the landing page; whether it is paid is inferable but **not stated, so treat as unverified.**

**Accessories.** Athletic tape 3-pack **$30**. No mouthguards, no mats. Otherwise curated co-brands: an anti-bacterial fabric spray with retaW ($35), Klean Kanteen cup ($23), Nalgene bottle ($50), **a Pilot erasable pen + Rhodia notepad set ($25, sold out)**, wrestling socks ($20, sold out), grappler-styled skate shoes ($130), sparring gloves ($90), medicine ball ($139). Credible taste-extension. The sold-out notepad set is directly relevant — see §5.4.

**Unverified.** Shoyoroll publishes no founding date and no ownership anywhere on its own site. Third-party sources claim 2006 and a named founder; **not verified from a primary source**. Community gi databases (gi.lol) record drop times that *precede* the store's own publish timestamps for the same product — the two records disagree; store data was treated as primary.

### Albino & Preto

**Positioning.** A collaboration-driven jiu-jitsu lifestyle house that treats the gi as a canvas for cultural licensing — roughly one release a week, most a co-sign with a music, toy, outdoor or fashion partner.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **Collaboration is the actual competency, and the roster is deep.** Live or recent: KISS, Tupac, Needles, Realtree, '47, Zebra Mats, Padmore & Barnes. The lookbook archive adds Nike SB, Wu-Tang Clan, Medicom BE@RBRICK, Dickies Japan, atmos, Saucony, NBA, Hot Wheels. ([lookbooks](https://www.albinoandpreto.com/blogs/lookbooks)) No other BJJ brand operates at this partner tier.
- **A legible price ladder:** Workwear $115–120 → BW Essential $145–155 → Comp/Classic $195–205 → in-house flagship $250 → collab $270–320.
- **Honest manufacturing split.** Apex rash guards stated Made in USA; gis marked Imported. ([Apex rashguard](https://www.albinoandpreto.com/products/a-p-apex-rashguard-black))
- **Zero discounting** — 0 of 143 live products below compare-at.

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** The name is the palette: white and black, mapped to belt ranks. The site is severe — no display typeface is loaded at all; platform default sans, navigation in caps, product titles as terse uppercase SKU strings. Colour, where it appears, is borrowed wholesale from the collaborator. House graphic language leans heraldic and workwear-uniform. Product copy is almost entirely spec-and-policy: two lines of fabric followed by several hundred words of order-change and anti-resale terms. The verbal identity lives in release names and lookbook headlines, written in trade-press house style. Register: fashion-industry deadpan — it assumes you already know why the partner matters.

**Price band (USD).** Rash guards $75–95 (in-house Apex $80; kids ranked $65); shorts $75–95; women's shorties and sports bra $60; tees $50 in-house, $55–65 collab; hoody $120; belts $35–40. Gi $115–$320. Outerwear reaches $386.

**Release model.** **103 of 143 live products published on a Wednesday** — essentially every week through 2026. Sellouts are total on desirable drops: every Needles piece, every Tupac piece, the 2026 Gi (0/15), the STP Gi (0/29). A membership apparatus is visible in the collection list: `2026reservemembersonly`, `early-access`, `privatesale`, `friends-and-family`. Two live **$1.00 placeholder SKUs** with a one-per-customer limit function as reservation tokens. Pre-orders run 5–6 months with hard cancellation deadlines.
**Unverified:** JP/EU "(SEEDING)" listings appear at roughly 40–45% of US retail (Apex Gi $110 vs $250). The mechanism is not explained anywhere on the site.

**Editorial.** Three separately branded content channels in the top navigation, and effectively nothing in them. `/blogs/lookbooks` holds 30 collaboration announcements in press-release voice, **stopping at 2024-05-20**. The channel whose nav label positions it as a magazine has 4 entries, three with **zero text body**. A third channel has **one** entry, also textless. `/pages/about` is two sentences. Nothing substantive published in over a year.

**Accessories.** One genuinely serious item plus curated goods. **Mats: A&P × Zebra roll-out mats, $650**, two 5 ft × 10 ft × 1¼ in panels, closed-cell cross-linked polyethylene core, bonded athletic vinyl, tatami texture — sold out. ([product](https://www.albinoandpreto.com/products/a-p-x-zebra-winner-s-circle-roll-out-mats-lr)) Zebra is the sport's incumbent mat manufacturer, so this is a credible co-sign rather than a badge on generic foam. **Finger tape $10.** No mouthguards. Everything else is lifestyle: KISS boxing gloves $75, Realtree boots $240, backpacks $180–250, a Tupac 7-inch $29, incense trays $50, soap $12, a license plate holder $15.

### Hyperfly

**Positioning.** A family-run San Diego outfitter competing on breadth and accessibility rather than scarcity — a permanently stocked ladder from beginner gi to competition flagship, plus kids', women's, licensed tie-ins and a large permanent sale rack.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **The best no-gi spec disclosure in the category.** Core Ranked rash guard: 80/20 poly-spandex, flatlock seams, four-way stretch, UV protection, unisex loose fit. Icon VI gi: 380 gsm pearl weave, three-piece construction, EVA foam collar, 7 oz ripstop pants, six-point loop system, IBJJF legal. ([Core Ranked](https://hyperfly.com/products/core-ranked-rash-guard), [Icon VI](https://hyperfly.com/products/icon-vi))
- **Price-ladder engineering.** Gi pants sold separately at $30 across five gi models, so a torn pair doesn't cost a whole gi. Entry rash guards $30.
- **Institutional channel.** Published wholesale and custom-academy programmes, ambassador scheme, gi donations — they sell *to gyms*.
- **Multilingual storefront** (pt / fr / es / de / ja variants).

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** The most conventionally sportswear-looking brand studied. The theme loads Inter and defines a very dark near-black surface token alongside a light one, flipping between stark dark and warm off-white. Graphics run to katakana lettering, a winged wordmark, chenille varsity patches and a heart symbol used as a standalone mark. Photography leans bright, high-key, product-on-athlete, plus licensed IP art (Godzilla, tokidoki pastels, MLB/NBA marks) rather than moody editorial. Verbal register is warm, first-person-plural and mantra-driven: a short phrase about heart being untrainable recurs as product tag, foundation name, blog title, page, patch, earring and doormat. Explicitly inclusive — the opposite of scarcity.

**Price band (USD).** Rash guards $30 (entry, from $60) → $65 (Core Ranked, never discounted) → $80–85 (ProComp Edge); collabs $65–75. Shorts $20–90. Spats $20–65. Gi $65 → $71.40 (from $119) → $160 → $195 → $250–275 collab. Belts $15–34. Tees $20–50.

**Release model.** Always-in-stock replenishment. Publish days spread evenly across the week — no drop rhythm. Core staples live for years and deeply stocked; items from 2013, 2016 and 2018 are still purchasable. **129 of 332 live products (~39%) below compare-at**, some by more than 40%, with Sale as a permanent nav item. Scarcity appears only in licensed capsules.

**Editorial.** The highest-volume live editorial of any apparel brand studied. `/blogs/hyperfly-blog` — 30 entries, most recent **2026-07-09**: a ~9,100-character IBJJF tournament explainer (2026-06-26), a ~7,000-character improvement piece, a ~6,800-character gi guide, a no-gi beginner checklist (2026-07-09), plus first-person athlete essays running 7,000–10,400 characters and a five-part serialised training programme. Technique posts exist historically.
**Honest caveat:** 2026 output is visibly SEO-shaped, and two of four blog channels have zero entries.
**Strategically important:** Hyperfly has claimed the *intellectual* register verbally. Their "Grapplism" concept (2024-11-05) frames grappling as an art movement and invokes brutalism as a reference — very close to Guard Theory's altitude. They deliver it as **t-shirts, a hoodie and a blog post**, not as technical substance. ([Grapplism](https://hyperfly.com/blogs/hyperfly-blog/introducing-grapplism-the-unified-art-and-philosophy-of-grappling))

**Accessories — widest adjacency set, mostly credible.**
- **Mouthguards: $35 each**, two designs, **made by Damage Control** (a specialist) with a stated dental warranty, marked final sale. ([Do Or Die](https://hyperfly.com/products/do-or-die-mouthguard), [Stay Fly](https://hyperfly.com/products/stay-fly-mouthguard))
- **Grip trainer $25** — a lapel/sleeve attachment with 450 gsm and 750 gsm sides and a 200 lb limit. The most convincingly sport-specific accessory in the whole survey. ([Grip-Tech](https://hyperfly.com/products/hyperfly-grip-tech))
- Hand wraps $18–40. **No athletic tape. No mats.** Bags $15–189.
- **Filler tail:** socks $7.50, beach towels, a $50 doormat, enamel pins, logo earrings $55–60.

**Self-reported.** Their about page states founding in 2011 in San Diego by two named founders, starting in a garage, with three of their children now operating the brand. Brand's own claim, not independently verified. ([about](https://hyperfly.com/pages/about-us))

### Half Sumo

**Positioning.** A Brooklyn design studio that happens to make fight gear — illustrated, story-led capsule collections rooted in Japanese tattoo and folklore imagery, sold as collectibles.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **Illustration is the core competency and it is legitimately theirs.** Every capsule is built on original hand-drawn irezumi-style artwork, and the same art is sold as **prints ($50–85)**. That is a designer's business model.
- **Construction detail with a real design idea.** The Mizu Dogi: custom-dyed navy, 375 gsm pearl weave jacket, 12 oz brushed cotton pants, sublimated artwork inside the collar, and a large soft interior print **designed to fade with age**. ([Mizu Dogi](https://halfsumo.com/products/mizu-dogi-navy-1)) Deliberate patina is distinctive in a category obsessed with print permanence.
- **Honest about a compromise.** Their rash guard copy states men's and women's cuts differ minimally because the fabric stretches, and advises women to size down. ([Mizu Kraken](https://halfsumo.com/products/mizu-kraken-rashguard)) Rare candour.
- **Collaborations punch above their size:** adidas, Coca-Cola, Gibson, Trivium, Megadeth, and official gear for the Renzo Gracie Academy. ([collabs](https://halfsumo.com/pages/collabs))

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** The most graphically maximal brand studied and the only illustration-first identity. Japanese tattoo flash at apparel scale — oni masks, tengu, koi, kraken tentacles, demon knights, kabuto helmets, dragons — in heavy black linework with limited flat spot colour, laid across the garment rather than printed as a chest logo. A clean humanist sans on plain white keeps the chrome quiet so the artwork carries. Naming is transliterated Japanese used systematically as taxonomy, with capsules framed as concepts. Copy voice is the loosest and funniest in the category. Register: independent, warm, slightly chaotic, unmistakably not corporate.

**Price band (USD).** Rash guards $75 standard, $59 lighter line; team jersey $85; grappling shorts $65; compression shorts $55; board shorts $50; spats $49–60; belts $45–55; tees $34–45; hoodie $70–75; dogi **$220**, $250 collab; backpacks $50–150; art prints $50–85.

**Release model.** Weekly capsules — 63 of 150 live products published on a **Thursday**, across 35 distinct 2026 release dates. Sell-through is **partial rather than total**, a healthier signal for a small brand than instant sellouts. Variant depth is genuinely thin (7 sizes vs Shoyoroll's 32–48). ~15% below compare-at with a clearance collection in the nav.

**Editorial.** Effectively none — the single blog renders as containing no articles. The nearest thing is a well-written ~600-word origin page and archived capsule lookbooks. **The storytelling happens entirely inside product descriptions, which are the best-written product descriptions in the category.** As a content operation it does not exist.

**Accessories.** **Hand wraps are a genuine line** — eleven individually illustrated designs at **$15**, plus bundles at $75. Applying the illustration engine to a consumable is their smartest merchandising idea. ([hand wraps](https://halfsumo.com/collections/hand-wraps)) Boxing gloves $99, belts $45–55. **No mouthguards, no tape, no mats.** Long filler tail: skateboard deck $75, jigsaw puzzle $40, keychains, pins, stickers, socks, beanies, underwear, plus collections for watches, gaming and Hawaiian shirts.

**Self-reported.** Two founders named (an Italian designer and Kyokushin competitor, and a Renzo Gracie black belt); collective started late 2018 with a 100-piece kimono run; a COVID-era programme designing free gear for 100+ academies. Brand-stated, not verified. ([about](https://halfsumo.com/pages/about))

### VHTS

**Positioning.** A small Brooklyn label running BJJ gear on a seasonal streetwear calendar — named collections, season codes, sell-outs and pre-orders rather than restocked staples.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **The most disciplined release calendar in the category.** Everything is tagged to a season (S/S 26, F/W 25, "Season 02/03") or a named series, making archive and recency legible at a glance.
- **Real collection identity** — series carry coherently across rash guard, shorts and sometimes gi as coordinated kits.
- **Longevity** in a segment that churns, with a high-end athlete roster.

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** **The closest existing brand to Guard Theory's stated aesthetic, and therefore the most important one to differentiate from.** The site is stark: white ground, black type, product grid. The *products* skew earthy and muted — bone, clay, cypress, sable, lichen grey, oak, charcoal, a warm off-white. Belt-rank colourways are the exception, not the house look. Product titles use season prefixes and quoted series names, reading like a fashion lookbook index rather than a fight-gear SKU list. Motifs are **texture-led rather than illustration-led** — waffle, mesh, translucence, camo, blur, ghost — unusual in a category that defaults to graphics. Tone is terse and slightly aphoristic; the brand name doubles as its ethos. Low on hype adjectives, high on naming. Photography unobserved.

**Price band (USD).** Rash guards **$65–$94** — floor is a Zero-G ranked staple at $65, bulk $83–$91, a Black Label line from $94. Shorts **$75–$93** — Zero-G $75, mainline $85–91, camo and special editions $93. **Spats: none in the current catalogue.** Tees $39 (mesh) and $55 (premium cotton); hoodies $105–115. ([combat gear](https://www.vhtsny.com/combat-gear), [combat shorts](https://www.vhtsny.com/combat-shorts))

**Release model.** Strongly drop-based. A large minority of rash guard SKUs show **Sold Out**, and several are **pre-order with a stated ~50–55 day wait**. Only the Zero-G and Ranked staples behave like continuity products.

**Editorial.** **None.** Navigation is Home / Shop / Sale / About / Cart — no blog, journal or technique section. Content effort lives on Instagram.

**Accessories.** Narrow and on purpose. **Finger tape, logo-printed, $12.** Belts $50–55. No mouthguards, no mats, no bags. ([accessories](https://www.vhtsny.com/accessories))

**Unverified/conflicting.** VHTS's own About page gives a Brooklyn address but **no founder name and no founding date**. European retailer bios state founded 2013 by a named founder; a 2014 gym blog post agrees. **Retailer-sourced, unverified by the brand.**

### Gaidama

**Positioning.** A founder-led, women-first grappling brand that grew outward into men's and kids' lines, selling on fit engineering and character-driven artwork rather than scarcity.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **The clearest origin logic in the category** — built by a practitioner who could not get women's gear that fit, then scaled outward, documented on their own site.
- **Genuinely broad segment coverage** — women's, men's, boys', kids', with IBJJF-legal ranked options across all.
- **Construction detail is specific** — built-in liners, custom waistbands, vented underarms. They name what they changed.

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** Dark site chrome deliberately receding so saturated product art can carry — teal, pink, lavender, OD green, burgundy, clay. Clean modern sans; heavy pipe-delimited taxonomy in product titles, reading systematic and catalogue-like. A fox is the recurring brand animal, alongside an East-Asian-referencing set (koi, sakura, a Musashi line, a ninja-suit product name), cyber/neon lines, and Latin/Day-of-the-Dead nods — plus a large seam of **joke-slogan graphics** on tees and stickers. Tone runs two registers in parallel: engineering-speak on technical pages, warm in-joke club voice on apparel. That split *is* the signature. Photography unobserved.

**Price band (USD).** Men's no-gi: rash guards $75, shorts $75 list. Women's: rash guards $75, shorts $75, **spats $85**. Tees $35 list, long-sleeve $40, tank $30.
**Effective prices are materially below list.** Clearance and exchange-only items ran **$22.50–$67.50**; a tee at **$8.75 (75% off)**; a cap at $10.00. A **30% Off Overstock Sale** collection and an accessories sale collection are **permanent navigation items**. ([men's no-gi](https://gaidama.com/collections/mens-nogi-jiu-jitsu-gear), [clearance](https://gaidama.com/collections/clearance))

**Release model.** Always-in-stock core with rotation. Nav includes "Back in Stock" and "Everything But Pre-Orders" — restock-brand information architecture. Scarcity comes from clearance and discontinuation, not limited batches.

**Editorial.** Real but **brand journalism, not technique**. `/blogs/news` carries founder interviews and year-in-review posts (2026-01-05, 2025-02-07, 2024-06-27, 2024-04-26, 2024-01-27, 2024-01-01, 2023-03-04) plus one topical piece on women in jiu-jitsu. **No technique content on-site.** ([blog](https://gaidama.com/blogs/news))

**Accessories.** Broad, mixed credibility. Credible: 36L backpack $135, 2-in-1 duffel $135, belt bag $40, gi belt $40, US-made socks $15, **ranked hair ribbon $12** (a genuinely thoughtful women's-grappling item nobody else makes). Filler-adjacent: 15+ sticker designs at $4 (clearing at $1), patches $8 (clearing at $1), caps $40 marked to $10. **No mouthguards, no tape, no mats.**

**Unverified.** Founder named on their own site. **No founding year stated anywhere on the site.** Repeated revenue and timeline figures are brand/PR-sourced and unverified.

### Phalanx

**Naming ambiguity — flagged.** This brand runs under at least three names across its store, LinkedIn, Facebook and social handles. A separate `phalanxathletics.com` appears in search but would not resolve (HTTP 422); **we cannot confirm** whether it is the same entity, a legacy domain, or unrelated. The canonical live store is `phalanxfc.com`.

**Positioning.** The old-guard no-gi specialist — claims to predate the no-gi boom and stakes its identity on technical fight shorts and rash guards rather than gis or fashion.

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **The narrowest catalogue in the survey, and no gis at all.** That focus *is* the positioning, and it is the only brand here that is structurally no-gi-only.
- **Kit bundling is a real merchandising strength**: matched rash-guard-plus-shorts kits at $129 against roughly $109–119 bought separately, bag-and-kit bundles at $179, and a free-shipping threshold set at exactly $129 — one kit. Deliberate.
- Full family coverage in a small line.

**Visual/verbal signature (described).** The darkest and coldest palette in the survey, with colourway names doing the work — vanta, dustcore, frostcore, ironcore, onyx, smoke, bone, atlantic. Near-total absence of bright colour. Product names are **all-caps throughout**, giving the catalogue a stencil/roster feel before you see an image. Two motif systems stack: **military formation** (the brand name is a Greek infantry block; a formations sub-brand extends it) and **American founding iconography** (1776, Liberty, Founders, Continental, We The People, plus a nationals series). Overlaid on both is a **memento mori / stoic-Latin** streak. Tone is clipped, imperative, martial; duration and endurance are the recurring claims; community is framed as an army. Photography unobserved.

**Price band (USD).** Rash guards **$44–$64.50**; fight shorts **$44–$64.50**; kits $109–$129; tees **$35 flat**. **No spats. No gis.** ([all products](https://phalanxfc.com/collections/all-products)) The lowest premium-tier pricing in the survey.

**Release model.** Mostly evergreen: core colourways in stock, pre-orders for national capsules, one accessory badged limited edition, permanent sale section. A restock brand using pre-order for capsules — the structural opposite of VHTS.

**Editorial.** **None.** `/blogs/news` **404s** and the about-us path 404s. The only content surfaces are a reviews page and a hashtag feed.

**Accessories.** Bags-led and reasonably credible. A **pearl-weave duffle at $69.50–79.50** — using gi fabric for a gear bag is a genuinely on-brand material idea and the strongest accessory concept in this set. Belts $24 (cheapest observed). Limited hat $34, flag $28, stickers $5. **No mouthguards, no tape, no mats.**

**Conflicting founding data.** Their own About page states 2012 and a named founder; their homepage simultaneously claims 14-plus years, implying earlier; LinkedIn lists 2011 and 11–50 employees. **Founding year, HQ and headcount are all unresolved.** Their claim to be the longest-standing no-gi company is a self-assertion that could not be corroborated.

### Level Black (LXB)

**Domain correction.** The live site is **`levelxblack.com`** (UK storefront `levelxblack.uk`), trading as LXB / Level Black. ### Scramble

**Positioning.** A UK label that fuses Japanese fight-culture graphic sensibility with an unusually literal commitment to the sport's infrastructure — publishing, gyms, camps and adjacency to a professional promotion — such that the apparel reads as the merchandising arm of a broader cultural operation rather than the other way round. **This is the single most important brand in this document for Guard Theory to understand.**

**What they genuinely do well.**
- **Material honesty at the gi tier** — 100% cotton stated in adult gis rather than poly blends, with a clean ladder from entry gi (free white belt included) to ultra-premium collaboration kimonos. ([gi range](https://scramblestuff.us/c/bjj-gi-kimonos/))

([Senshu rashguard](https://scramblestuff.com/product/senshu-rashguard-26-black/))

### Future Kimonos

**Positioning.** A made-to-order, athlete-signature no-gi kit house built around named grapplers and themed graphic collections, where the core mechanic is 4–6 week production and personalisation rather than shelf stock.

**Three findings that overturn the common assumption about this brand:**
1. **It sells no gis.** Despite the name, there is **zero gi product in the live catalogue** — `/collections/gis`, `/collections/kimonos` and `/collections/pro-series-kimono` all **404**, and none of the 43 live collections is a gi collection. No waitlist, no notify-me, no back-in-stock signup, no pre-order labelling.
3. **Nothing is ever sold at its stated price. 100% of variants carry a struck-through compare-at** of $129.00 — a figure charged on **zero** variants.

- **Set logic.** Almost every design ships as a matched family — men's SS and LS rash guard, fight shorts, women's SS and LS, women's spats — so a competitor can buy a coordinated kit in one theme.
- **Women's depth is structural, not tokenistic** — roughly **86 of 349 SKUs** across distinct women's product types.
- **Size range XS–4XL** on men's short-sleeve rash guards.
- **Athlete design input is genuine, not a logo slap** — signature lines are structurally separate collections themed on the athlete's identity, plus an ADCC federation series.

## 2. Category conventions — what is over-used

These are the defaults. A new brand that does any of them will read as one of thirteen.

### 2.1 The monochrome-plus-rank-stripe uniform is partly a rulebook artefact

This matters more than it looks. **IBJJF no-gi rules require the rash guard to be black, white, or black-and-white, and to carry at least 10% of the competitor's belt-rank colour**, with a narrow red allowance for black belts only. ([BJJ Heroes summary](https://www.bjjheroes.com/bjj-news/ibjjf-no-gi-uniform-requirements); corroborated by [Scramble's explainer](https://scramblestuff.com/ibjjf-legal-ranked-rashguards-all-you-need-to-know/))

Consequences:
- **Every serious brand ships a "Ranked" SKU** — Hyperfly, Scramble, VHTS, Gaidama, Progress, Moya's five-colour flagship, plus Fuji and Hayabusa outside this set. **"Ranked" is now a generic product-category word, not a brand asset.**
- The black/white/grey default is therefore **half compliance, half taste**. A restrained monochrome palette is not automatically differentiating — it is the legal baseline.
- **Implication for Guard Theory's colour vetoes:** the no-red rule costs essentially nothing, because red is only permitted as a small black-belt allowance anyway. The restrained palette is aligned with the rulebook rather than fighting it. **Differentiation must come from everything other than colour.**

### 2.2 Japanese and East-Asian signifiers as default product taxonomy

Used as the naming *system*, not as an occasional reference:
- **Half Sumo** — a full transliterated-Japanese taxonomy with irezumi-style illustration as the entire visual identity.
- **Scramble** — product families named for a beef grade, a blade type, a finger; souvenir-jacket embroidery codes; a heritage tee line on canonical Japanese MMA bouts.
- **Gaidama** — a fox as brand animal plus koi, sakura, a Musashi line and a ninja-suit product name.
- **Hyperfly** — katakana lettering, a Japan-year rash guard, tokidoki and Godzilla licences.
- **VHTS** — Japanese-referenced special editions.
- **Future Kimonos** — samurai and kintsugi references, neo-Tokyo framing, and a brand name that no longer matches its catalogue.

Recurring motif stack across the category: **oni and hannya masks, dragons and serpents, samurai and kabuto helmets, koi, tattoo/irezumi linework, skulls.** So saturated it now reads as category wallpaper.

### 2.3 The second stack: military, stoic and warrior signifiers

Where a brand isn't doing Japan, it is usually doing one of:
- **Special-operations and formation naming** — Phalanx (a Greek infantry block, a formations sub-brand, vanta/dustcore/ironcore colourways, 1776 and founding-era iconography); LXB (operation codenames, desert camo, a special-forces charity tie).
- **Memento mori, Latin mottoes, stoicism** — Phalanx runs this explicitly.
- **Heart, grit and warrior mantras** — Hyperfly's entire verbal identity is built on a short untrainable-heart phrase repeated across product, foundation, blog, patch and doormat; Future Kimonos runs glory-and-dominance copy in all-caps.

**Guard Theory's veto on warrior clichés removes it from both stacks at once. That is unusual, and it is most of the opportunity.**

### 2.4 Copy patterns

- **Spec-then-nothing.** Two lines of fabric composition followed by policy text (A&P) or nothing at all.
- **Philosophy-as-merch.** Hyperfly's Grapplism is the clearest case: an art-movement framing invoking brutalism, shipped as tees and a hoodie. **The intellectual register is being claimed verbally without technical substance behind it — the closest verbal adjacency to Guard Theory in the category, and it is only skin-deep.**
- **Process and journey language.** Ubiquitous and interchangeable.
- **No About page, or a two-sentence one.** Only Hyperfly, Half Sumo, Scramble, Progress and Tatami publish real histories. Shoyoroll publishes none; A&P's is two sentences; **Future Kimonos' about page 404s entirely**; VHTS, Gaidama, LXB and Moya state no founding date and Moya names no founder.

### 2.5 Product naming conventions

- **Batch and version numbers** as collectible identity — Shoyoroll batch-numbered kimono handles, A&P batches catalogued at gi.lol, Hyperfly's numbered Icon/ProComp generations, VHTS "Type 02" / "Season 02/03", Future's v1.0 and 2099-style year codes.
- **Seasonal and year codes** — VHTS S/S and F/W prefixes; Moya's two-digit year prefix; Progress's heritage year numerals used as graphic elements.
- **Colourway names doing brand work** where the graphic can't — Phalanx (vanta, dustcore, frostcore, ironcore, bone); VHTS (bone, sable, lichen grey); Progress (khaki, a desaturated teal-grey).
- **Nationality series** — VHTS, Phalanx, Hyperfly and Moya all independently arrived at this.

### 2.6 Launch and commerce mechanics

- **Fixed weekly drop days.** A&P Wednesday (103/143 products), Half Sumo Thursday (63/150), Shoyoroll Friday (58/104). The day of the week *is* the ritual.
- **Total sellout as the success signal**, with an active resale market and community batch databases archiving what you missed.
- **Gated tiers** — Shoyoroll's login-walled Reserve and Be First; A&P's members-only, early-access, private-sale and friends-and-family collections plus $1.00 reservation-token SKUs.
- **Pre-order with long lead times** — A&P at 5–6 months with hard cancellation deadlines; VHTS at ~50–55 days; Future Kimonos at 4–6 weeks as its *entire* model.
- **Only one genuine non-drop scarcity mechanic exists in the whole set** — Scramble's one-per-customer mystery no-gi bundle, discount-codes-excluded, sold until gone.
- **At the other pole: permanent discounting.** Future Kimonos with 100% of variants permanently marked down against a compare-at price never charged; Hyperfly ~39% of catalogue; Tatami running flash sales, clearance *and* category outlets concurrently; Gaidama with permanent overstock and observed 75%-off items; Scramble with a standing 33-item sale at 30–50%. Category Black Friday depth commonly runs 20–70%. ([discount roundup](https://bjjequipment.com/black-friday-bjj-deals/))
- **The category is bimodal.** Never-discount scarcity houses (Shoyoroll and A&P at literally 0%) versus always-discount volume brands. **Only two brands occupy a credible middle: Moya (no sale section at all) and Progress (surgical 50% end-of-line only). That middle is nearly empty.**
- **Licensed IP collaboration is a core growth lever** — music (KISS, Tupac, Wu-Tang, Megadeth, Trivium, Gibson), toys and anime (BE@RBRICK, Godzilla, tokidoki, Peanuts, Sanrio), sportswear and leagues (Nike SB, adidas, Saucony, MLB, NBA), outdoor and fashion (Realtree, Needles, Dickies).

### 2.7 Sublimated all-over print as the default construction

Sublimation is near-universal for rash guards and is heavily promoted as a durability feature. One commerce-aggregator source claims sublimation and logo personalisation appear in roughly 90% of listings — **aggregator-sourced, directional not precise**. ([Accio trend page](https://www.accio.com/t-v2/business/top-selling-jiu-jitsu-rashguard)) The practical effect: **the category has been designing for a printing process that rewards maximal graphics**, which is why restraint is rare.

### 2.8 What is conspicuously absent across the whole category

- **Only two of thirteen sell a mouthguard** — Hyperfly ($35, made by Damage Control) and Scramble (£15).
- **Only one sells mats** — A&P, a single $650 Zebra co-brand, sold out.
- **Four sell no spats at all** — Phalanx, LXB, VHTS and Moya (`/spats/` 404s).
- **Nobody publishes a structured technique library.** Only Progress publishes athlete-authored technique breakdowns, and only occasionally; Scramble's technique category has been dormant since **February 2019**; Shoyoroll's technique series is video embeds with no article text.
- **Almost nobody sustains editorial cadence.** Five brands publish nothing at all.

## 3. White space — ranked by defensibility

Ranked by how hard each gap would be for an incumbent to close if Guard Theory occupied it.

### Tier 1 — structurally defensible (hard to copy, compounding)

**1. A structured technique library as a browsable text-and-diagram reference.**
This is the clearest gap in the entire category. The instructional market is overwhelmingly video and overwhelmingly paid — subscriptions at $12.99–$25/month per instructor, individual instructionals listed around $197 with constant heavy discounting. ([pricing summary](https://bjjmore.com/submeta-review/)) Text/diagram references exist but sit **entirely outside apparel**: jiujitsu.org (184 techniques across guard/submissions/takedowns/escapes with gi/no-gi filtering, free), BJJ Mental Models (100+ concept entries, text, part-gated), flowchart tools, and Artechoke's hybrid text-plus-animated-GIF instructionals. ([jiujitsu.org](https://www.jiujitsu.org/), [BJJ Mental Models](https://www.bjjmentalmodels.com/database), [Artechoke](https://artechokemedia.com/))
**Why defensible:** a large, slow, unglamorous build no apparel brand has attempted, which accrues search authority and repeat visits between purchases.
**Two threats to watch, both real:** Shoyoroll's Universal Grappling System is instructor-facing pedagogy sold in limited cycles, currently sold out, with seminars in three European capitals. And **Scramble's co-founder is publicly associated with Techniqly, an instructional video platform** — meaning the category's best writer has already routed his technique ambitions into a separate venture. Guard Theory would be the first to put technique *back inside* an apparel brand, in text.

**2. A genuinely technical written journal with real publishing cadence.**
Editorial quality exists in pockets — Scramble's essays are excellent, Moya's newsroom is frequent, Progress's athlete-authored technique is the highest-credibility format anyone uses, Hyperfly has volume. But **no single brand has quality, cadence and technical depth together**, and the failure pattern is stark: A&P built three content channels and stopped in 2024; Shoyoroll abandoned its technique series in 2022 and its blog in 2025; **Tatami's blog has been silent for roughly four years**; Scramble's own technique category died in 2019; Inverted Gear — historically the apparel brand with the most genuine editorial including guest technical writers — is dormant since 2025. ([Inverted Gear blog](https://www.invertedgear.com/blogs/inverted-gear-blog))
**Why defensible:** editorial compounds, and the incumbents' revealed behaviour is that they cannot sustain it.
**Caveat:** only defensible if the cadence holds. A dormant journal is worse than no journal.

**3. Restraint executed with actual craft, in a category built for maximal print.**
Sublimation economics push everyone toward all-over graphics. Only VHTS and Progress are meaningfully restrained — VHTS texture-led (waffle, mesh, translucence), Progress via a muted utilitarian palette with louder illustration ring-fenced into collabs.
**Why defensible:** a taste position backed by production discipline. Competitors can copy a colourway in one drop; they cannot easily copy an art direction that requires saying no to their own printing advantage.
**Note:** VHTS owns quiet-and-earthy at $65–94 and Progress owns muted-utilitarian at £32–48. **Differentiation against both must come from *system* — a legible, designed product architecture — not from quietness alone.**

### Tier 2 — defensible with sustained effort

**4. Honest, specific fit and sizing information.**
Shoyoroll publishes shrinkage by colour and cuts three fit blocks per size; Progress publishes weight-banded sizing and warns its garments run tight; Half Sumo openly admits its women's cut barely differs; Scramble warns one line runs small; Hyperfly discloses composition properly. **Everyone else is vague, and nobody publishes actual garment measurements.**
**Why it works:** the highest-trust, lowest-cost signal of technical seriousness, and it directly reduces returns.

**5. A credible middle on price and promotion.**
Only **two** of thirteen brands hold a defensible pricing position: Moya (no sale section exists at all) and Progress (surgical flat-50% on end-of-line only). Everything else is either 0%-discount scarcity or permanent markdown — with Future Kimonos the extreme case, where a $129 anchor price has been charged on **zero** variants.
**Why defensible:** a business-model commitment, not a design choice. Volume brands cannot follow without breaking their unit economics.

**6. No-gi-first as an actual structural commitment.**
Only Phalanx is structurally no-gi-only, and it prices at the bottom of the premium band ($44–64.50) with martial-patriotic art direction. Future Kimonos is no-gi-only by attrition rather than intent — it discontinued gis and kept the name. Everyone else treats no-gi as the cheaper sibling of the gi business.
**Why defensible:** it forces different product decisions — spats, shorts fit, fabric weight, seam placement — that a gi-led brand won't prioritise.

**7. Spats as a properly designed premium category.**
**Four brands carry none at all** — Phalanx, LXB, VHTS and Moya. Where they exist the range is Progress €22–52, Half Sumo $49–60, Scramble £40, Gaidama $85 (women's). In a discipline where leg entanglements dominate, this is under-engineered — and the gap is specifically at the premium, no-gi-specialist end.

### Tier 3 — real but shallower moats

**8. A grappling-specific consumables line done credibly.** Tape is proven across six brands; **mouthguards are open at eleven of thirteen**. See §5.

**9. Genuine women's no-gi engineering rather than a colourway.** Gaidama has claimed this ground first and credibly; Future Kimonos has real structural depth (86 of 349 SKUs); Progress has proper women's product; Half Sumo openly admits it hasn't. Defensible only with real pattern work.

**10. Supply-chain transparency, not "eco" adjectives.** Tatami already runs a GRS-approved RPET line with specific composition and CO₂ claims. The gap is **named factory, country of make, garment lifespan** — which nobody does. A&P's honest Made-in-USA-vs-Imported split is the closest anyone gets.

### Explicitly *not* white space
- Japanese motifs, samurai/oni/dragon iconography, tattoo linework.
- Military and special-operations naming; memento mori and stoic Latin.
- Warrior, heart, grit and glory copy.
- Nationality and flag capsules — four brands already run them.
- Licensed music and anime collaborations — the tier is occupied by A&P and Hyperfly.
- Batch numbers and weekly drop days — owned rituals with a decade of community infrastructure.
- Brand-journalism blogs about the brand itself — Gaidama has that, and Moya does the news version better.

### 4.2 The pattern

**Five of thirteen brands publish nothing. Four more built editorial channels and let them decay** — A&P (stopped 2024), Shoyoroll (technique 2022, blog 2025), Tatami (2022, roughly four years silent), and Scramble's own technique vertical (2019). Inverted Gear, historically the most editorially serious apparel brand in BJJ, has been dormant since 2025.

**The conclusion is not "editorial is easy here." It is: editorial is *unoccupied* because it is hard to sustain, and almost every attempt has decayed.** That is what makes it defensible — and it is also the single biggest execution risk.

**The second pattern is fragmentation.** No brand holds all three of quality, cadence and technical depth:
- Scramble has **quality** without cadence, and its technique has migrated to a separate company.
- Moya has **cadence** without technique.
- Progress has **technique** without verified depth or a clear editorial identity.
- Hyperfly has **volume** without a point of view.
**The unoccupied position is all three at once.**

### 4.3 Where the good BJJ writing actually lives — outside apparel

Guard Theory's Journal will be judged against these, not against brand blogs:
- **Artechoke Media** — text-first instructionals combining descriptive writing, multi-angle animated GIFs and embedded video, with theory sections and common-mistake breakdowns. The most direct precedent for a written technique library. ([artechokemedia.com](https://artechokemedia.com/))
- **BJJ Mental Models** — 100+ text entries organised as *concepts* (learning, mechanical, social, strategic) rather than techniques, free/premium split. ([database](https://www.bjjmentalmodels.com/database)) **This is the closest existing thing to Guard Theory's thesis that great grappling is not random** — and it is a media business with no apparel.
- **jiujitsu.org** — a free structured library of 184 techniques across four categories with gi/no-gi filtering and written breakdowns. ([jiujitsu.org](https://www.jiujitsu.org/))
- **Grapplearts** and **Aesopian** — long-running written analysis and one of the longest-running written BJJ resources.
- **Techniqly** — an instructional video platform associated with Scramble's co-founder. ([techniqly.com](https://techniqly.com/pc/instructional-videos/))
- **Trade press:** Jits Magazine (digital, established 2012), Jiu-Jitsu Magazine, BJJ Heroes for history and rules reference. Print titles have largely moved digital.

### 4.4 The precedent worth studying — from outside BJJ entirely

**Tracksmith's Meter** is the strongest structural model: a quarterly magazine covering running culture that carries **no product promotion and no ads**, sold as a product in its own right rather than given away as marketing. ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/meet-meter-magazine-thats-taking-anti-nike-approach-running/), [product page](https://www.tracksmith.com/products/meter-magazine-general))

The transferable lesson is not "make a magazine." It is: **the editorial must be good enough that it would exist without the products, and it must not sell.** Every failed brand blog above failed by becoming product marketing.

### 4.5 Competitive threats to flag

1. **Scramble is the real cultural competitor, not the streetwear brands.** They already have the best writing, a gym network, a physical academy, camps, and a founder adjacent to both a professional promotion and an instructional platform. If they ever restarted their technique vertical with cadence, they would be very hard to beat. **Their revealed weakness is exactly that: they let it die in 2019 and routed it elsewhere.**
2. **Shoyoroll has already proved a premium grappling brand can sell *thinking about jiu-jitsu*** — instructor-facing curriculum, limited cycles, sold out, seminars in London, Paris and Milan. Guard Theory is not entering an empty field at the top end.
3. **Progress owns the single most credible editorial format** — technique written by a world champion who wears the kit. That is very hard to out-authority without comparable athletes.

### 5.2 Mouthguards — the widest open lane, but harder than it looks

**Only two of thirteen sell one:**
- **Hyperfly, $35**, two designs — **made by Damage Control**, a specialist, with a stated dental warranty, marked final sale (correct handling for a hygiene product). ([Do Or Die](https://hyperfly.com/products/do-or-die-mouthguard))
- **Scramble, £15.** ([accessories](https://scramblestuff.com/c/accessories/))

Eleven brands sell none — including Progress, Moya and Tatami, all of which have credible accessory ranges and simply skip **the single most-replaced protective item in the sport.**

The independent field splits cleanly:
- **Custom, impression-kit-based:** Impact Mouthguards, Damage Control, Gladiator Guards, GuardLab, TRUFIT — all offering BJJ-specific thin-profile guards emphasising breathing and speech. **Prices not observed** (product pages did not display pricing at the pages fetched). ([Impact BJJ page](https://www.impactmouthguards.com/pages/jiu-jitsu-mouthguards), [Damage Control BJJ page](https://www.dcmouthguards.com/pages/jiu-jitsu-mouthguards))
- **Boil-and-bite commodity:** Sanabul, Venum, Hayabusa, Shock Doctor, generally reported at **$3–$30**. ([category roundup](https://jiuoss.com/best-bjj-mouthguard/) — third-party review site, indicative only)

**Assessment:** a mouthguard is a **credible extension only via a named specialist partner**, exactly as Hyperfly did. Own-brand boil-and-bite would read as filler and carries real product-liability and hygiene exposure. The genuine opening is a properly designed, low-profile grappling guard with a named manufacturer, honest protection claims, and a case worth keeping.

### 5.3 Mats — the least credible extension, with one exception that proves the rule

**Only one of thirteen sells mats: Albino & Preto, and only as a co-branded product with Zebra** — the sport's incumbent manufacturer — at **$650** for two 5 ft × 10 ft × 1¼ in panels with a closed-cell cross-linked polyethylene core and bonded athletic vinyl cover. Sold out. ([product](https://www.albinoandpreto.com/products/a-p-x-zebra-winner-s-circle-roll-out-mats-lr))

The mats market is a separate B2B industry with entrenched specialists — **Zebra, Dollamur, FUJI Mats, Swain.** Reference points: a Dollamur 5×10 home roll-out around **$349.99** (from $369.99); FUJI home roll-outs **from $379.00**. ([Dollamur](https://dollamur.com/flexi-rollr-home-ma-mma-bjj-mat-5x10-tatami.html), [FUJI](https://fujimats.com/collections/home-roll-outs))

**The one genuine apparel→mats success is FUJI**, where FUJI Mats operates as a facility-outfitting division under the same brand umbrella as FUJI Sports' gi and apparel business. ([FUJI Brands](https://fujimats.com/pages/fuji-brands)) That is a manufacturing and B2B distribution business, not an accessory SKU.

**Two disambiguations to avoid errors:** FUJI's "Tatami Series" mats are named for the *tatami surface texture*, not for Tatami Fightwear. And a Tatami-branded home roll-out mat is listed by at least one European retailer but **was not verified on Tatami's own storefront** — treat as unconfirmed.

**Assessment: mats are not a credible extension for a young apparel brand.** Capital-intensive, freight-heavy, dominated by specialists, and one $650 sold-out co-brand is the entire category precedent. **Guard Theory should not do mats.**

### 5.4 What the category does instead — and where the real opening is

The accessory tails divide sharply:
- **Credible and sport-specific:** Hyperfly's grip trainer ($25 — the most convincing accessory anyone sells), Scramble's grip trainers (£25) and training ball, Half Sumo's eleven-design hand-wrap line ($15 each), Phalanx's pearl-weave gi-fabric duffle ($69.50–79.50), **Progress's Legend Bag range segmented by what you train rather than by size** (£20–45 — the most BJJ-native merchandising idea in the survey), Gaidama's ranked hair ribbon ($12), tape across six brands.
- **Curated taste extensions that work** because a named specialist is attached: Shoyoroll's retaW fabric spray ($35) and Klean Kanteen/Nalgene co-brands; A&P's Zebra mats and Padmore & Barnes boots; Scramble's £2,950 collaboration e-bike (a deliberate lifestyle-house flex, not filler).
- **Straightforward filler:** license plate frames (A&P $15, LXB $20), flags, doormats ($50), jigsaw puzzles ($40), skateboard decks ($75), enamel pins, beach towels, incense trays ($50), logo earrings ($55–60), and long sticker-and-patch tails clearing at $1.

**The opening nobody has taken.** Shoyoroll sold a **co-branded erasable pen and notepad set at $25 — and it is sold out.** ([accessories](https://shoyoroll.com/collections/accessories)) That is the only *notational* object in the entire category, from the most design-literate brand in it, and the market cleared it. For a brand whose thesis is that great grappling is not random, a properly made **training notebook or logbook** is the most on-thesis physical accessory available — and it currently exists as a competitor's one-off rather than as an owned category.

## 6. Implications for Guard Theory

Twelve recommendations, ordered roughly by leverage.

**1. Lead with the Technique Library, not the Journal.**
The Library is the more defensible of the two. Nobody in the category has one; the field's text-and-diagram references all sit outside apparel; and the two brands closest to it have both routed technique *away* from themselves — Shoyoroll into a limited-cycle instructor curriculum, Scramble into a separate video company. **Being the first apparel brand to put structured technique back in-house, in text, is a clearer claim than "we have a good blog."**

**2. Organise the Library by system and decision, not by technique name.**
"Great grappling is not random" is a claim about structure. BJJ Mental Models already owns *concepts*; jiujitsu.org owns *technique enumeration* and does it free. The unclaimed middle is **positional decision trees and constraint logic** — why this entry, from this grip, against this reaction — which is also the thing an apparel brand can uniquely tie to no-gi specifics: no lapel, sweat and grip decay, leg-entanglement priority. Do not build a rival technique count; that ground is taken.

**3. Treat the Journal's cadence as the product, not its quality.**
Quality is achievable — Scramble proves a single good writer beats the category. **Every editorial attempt here has died of irregularity, not bad writing**: A&P stopped in 2024, Shoyoroll's technique series in 2022, Tatami's blog four years ago, Scramble's own technique vertical in 2019, Inverted Gear in 2025. Commit to a fixed, publicly stated cadence sustainable for 24 months — fortnightly is more defensible than weekly — and treat a missed slot as a defect.

**4. Make the Journal fail the marketing test on purpose.**
Adopt the Tracksmith/Meter principle: the writing must be good enough to exist without the products, and it must not sell. No product placement in essays, no gear round-ups under the same masthead, no SEO listicles. Put commercial content in a clearly separate channel. **Every failed brand blog above failed by becoming product marketing.**

**5. Assume the real competitors are Scramble, Shoyoroll and Progress — not the streetwear brands.**
Scramble has the best writing plus a gym network, an academy, camps and a founder adjacent to a pro promotion and an instructional platform. Shoyoroll already sells pedagogy at premium prices. Progress has technique written by a world champion who wears the kit. **Guard Theory's counter-position against all three is the same: open and always-available rather than gated, sold-out or cycle-limited; written and structured rather than video or in-person; free at the point of use.** Do not compete on scarcity with brands that invented it here.

**6. Do not differentiate on colour — differentiate on system.**
Monochrome plus a rank stripe is the IBJJF baseline, not a brand asset, and "Ranked" is a generic product word. The no-red veto costs nothing. VHTS already owns quiet-and-earthy at $65–94; Progress owns muted-utilitarian at £32–48. **Guard Theory's visual differentiation has to come from a legible product architecture** — a naming and numbering system, consistent garment nomenclature, published specifications — that reads as designed rather than decorated. Restraint alone is not a position; restraint plus a system is.

**7. Publish specifications and fit data as a trust weapon.**
Shoyoroll publishes shrinkage by colour and three fit blocks per size; Progress publishes weight-banded sizing and warns its gear runs tight; Scramble warns a line runs small; Half Sumo admits its women's cut barely differs. **Nobody publishes actual garment measurements.** Publish garment measurements, fabric weight and composition, seam construction, intended fit and expected lifespan on **every** product. Cheap, fully consistent with "intelligent and technical," lowers returns, and directly earns the credibility the Journal trades on.

**8. Price once, honestly, and never run an outlet.**
The category is bimodal and the middle is nearly empty — only Moya (no sale section at all) and Progress (surgical 50% end-of-line) hold a defensible position. The cautionary tale is Future Kimonos, where a $129 anchor has been charged on **zero** variants. Target the **$75–95** band for rash guards and shorts — level with VHTS and A&P, above Phalanx, Progress and Moya — and hold it. No invented RRP, no clearance, no Black Friday. **Restraint in commerce is the same argument as restraint in design, and it is verifiable in a way taste is not.**

**9. Choose availability over drops, and say why.**
Weekly drop days are owned rituals with a decade of community infrastructure — batch databases, resale markets, gated member tiers. Entering there means competing on someone else's terms with no archive. **Always-in-stock core, refreshed slowly, is the coherent choice for a brand arguing that good grappling is systematic rather than lucky** — and it removes the resale distortion that makes Shoyoroll and A&P products inaccessible to actual practitioners. Progress's never-out-of-stock core line, existing as a full horizontal system across every category, is the model to copy.

**10. Take spats seriously — the clearest single product gap.**
**Four of thirteen carry none at all** (Phalanx, LXB, VHTS, Moya), and where they exist the premium end is thin. In a no-gi-first discipline dominated by leg entanglements, a properly patterned spat with published specifications is a concrete, defensible product decision that also demonstrates the no-gi-first commitment.

**11. Accessories: tape yes, notebook yes, mouthguard only with a partner, mats never.**
- **Tape** is validated across six brands ($10–30 / £10) and reads as credible **only when fully specified** — width, length, roll count, fibre, adhesive. Follow Scramble's example, not A&P's.
- **A training notebook or logbook** is the most on-thesis object available. Shoyoroll's $25 co-branded pen-and-notepad set sold out and is the only notational product in the category. It also ties directly to the Journal and Library rather than sitting beside them.
- **Mouthguard** only via a named specialist manufacturer, as Hyperfly did with Damage Control. Eleven of thirteen brands skip the most-replaced item in the sport — but own-brand boil-and-bite is filler with liability attached.
- **Mats: do not.** One $650 co-brand is the entire category precedent; the field belongs to Zebra, Dollamur and FUJI, and the only successful crossover is a separate B2B manufacturing division.
- **Consider bags segmented by training mode**, as Progress does — genuinely BJJ-native thinking and cheap to copy well.
- Avoid the observed filler tail entirely: license plate frames, flags, doormats, puzzles, skate decks, pins, beach towels, dollar stickers.

**12. Sequence the Library and Journal to fill the gap between purchases — and instrument it.**
Apparel purchase frequency is low; the content is what makes the site worth returning to weekly. Two consequences: the Library should be **free and unauthenticated** (gating it replicates Shoyoroll's scarcity, which recommendation 5 argues against), and the Journal should be the reason the email list exists, not the discount code. Set an explicit rule for what the Journal will never publish — gear round-ups, listicles, warrior-mindset content, athlete-signing announcements dressed as features — and hold it.

### Three risks to hold in view

- **Editorial decay is the failure mode.** Nine of thirteen brands either publish nothing or let a channel rot. A visibly abandoned Journal would actively damage a brand whose whole claim is rigour. **Budget for the writer before budgeting for the photoshoot.**
- **Hyperfly has already claimed the intellectual register verbally.** Their Grapplism framing invokes art movements and brutalism and positions grappling as method and philosophy — very close to Guard Theory's altitude — but delivers it as tees and a blog post. **The position is only winnable by having the substance they don't.** A thin Journal and Library would read as a second Grapplism.
- **Progress's athlete-authored technique is the hardest format to beat.** A world and ADCC champion writing technique in the brand's own kit carries authority no in-house writer can manufacture. If Guard Theory's Library is written by a non-elite practitioner, its authority must come from **structure, clarity and honesty about uncertainty** rather than from credentials — and that should be an explicit editorial policy, not an accident.
