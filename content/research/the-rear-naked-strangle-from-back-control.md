## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **ADCC permits the can opener; UWW names it as an example of a prohibited neck crank.** A direct contradiction between two rule books. Printed as a contradiction in the sibling guillotine article and recorded in both `contestedNotes` sets. Not reconciled.
- **Choke vs strangle vocabulary.** Inconsistent across coaching traditions *and* across the rule books cited — UWW's own crank footnote says "rear naked choke". The article states the inconsistency, says which usage this site prefers, and says why, without claiming the other is wrong.
- **The English name.** Accounts of where "rear naked choke" comes from (a description of the absent gi grip; a mistranslation of *hadaka*) circulate widely. Nothing consulted settles it. The article says so; `contestedNotes` repeats it.
- **Body triangle scoring.** IBJJF advantage vs UWW/ADCC full points. Printed as a disagreement in the sibling turtle article.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following cut:**

- *Cut:* **every physiological statement.** No arteries, no airway, no blood flow, no unconsciousness, no timing, no "how it works" at the level of the body. The article describes an arrangement of limbs and what four rule books permit. This is a hard constraint from the commission and it should not be relaxed by a later editor "for clarity".
- *Cut:* the entire medical literature. ## 10. Editorial notes

- **The safety section is not decoration and is not compressible.** Release-on-tap, release-if-unresponsive, no last squeeze, no training on an unwilling partner, supervised room, agreed terms. If length pressure ever hits this article, take it out of §6, not §5.
- The article deliberately declines to explain *why* releasing matters. Explaining it would require the physiological claim the brief prohibits, and the instruction stands without the explanation.
- The order — position, then arm, then the crank line — is chosen so that a reader who stops early has read the position and not the finish.
- Cross-check: `src/content/technique/entries/blood-choke-versus-air-choke.ts` and `seat-belt-and-hooks.ts` cover neighbouring ground and are consistent with this article. That entry *does* mention carotid arteries; this article does not, per the tighter constraint on this commission. If the two are ever unified, the technique entry is the one carrying the older standard.
