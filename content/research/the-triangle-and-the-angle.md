## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **Where the crank line sits.** The four rule books word it differently and none defines it measurably. UWW's footnote is the only affirmative statement that neck pressure inside a choking technique is permitted. The article prints all of them and explicitly declines to synthesise a definition. Recorded in `contestedNotes`.
- **The IBJJF table's division columns.** The table is a grid of six division columns against 26 numbered techniques. The captions extract as text; the per-column marks are images and do **not**. Every attempt to assign a row to a division was abandoned. The article names techniques as appearing in the table and says in `contestedNotes` that it cannot say which divisions they apply to. **Do not "fix" this by guessing.**
- **Judo vs wrestling on the same shape.** Deliberately left as a contrast rather than resolved: it is the point of §5.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following cut:**

- *Cut:* everything physiological. No mention of what a strangle does to a person, no timelines, no mechanism. The strangle/crank distinction is carried entirely by rule-book wording.
- *Cut:* the claim that the triangle is among the most common submissions in competition. Unsourceable from anything consulted; the only frequency data located puts the heel hook first in a no-gi submission-only sample.
- *Cut:* any attribution of the triangle's origin or introduction to a named person or school.
- *Cut:* a planned assertion that the arm-in requirement exists "because of the risk to the neck". The rule books state the prohibition, not the reason. The article says a governing body treats it as a different action and stops there.
- *Cut:* every division assignment from the IBJJF illegal-moves table (see §3).
- *Cut:* a sentence claiming judo restricts *shime-waza* by age bracket. Plausible and probably true, but the age provisions are in the main body of the IJF SOR, not in Appendix D, and the main body was not obtained. Not asserted.
- *Cut:* "most triangles fail because of the angle" — a frequency claim with no source. Rewritten as a diagnostic procedure the reader can run.
