## 3. Contradictions and how each was handled

- **The definitions disagree on resistance.** "Zero resistance" (one version BJJ Fanatics reports), "minimal resistance" (Evolve 2024), light resistance with submissions caught and released (Easton; BJJ Fanatics' other version). The article prints the disagreement and uses it as evidence that no fixed definition exists.
- **One publisher, two meanings.** Evolve MMA uses flow for a kind of round (2024) and for relaxing inside any roll (2018). Both are quoted.
- **Challenge-skill balance: confirmed or not.** Peifer et al. say a meta-analysis (Fong et al. 2015) "confirmed the stability of challenge-skill balance as a condition of flow"; Abuhamdeh says flow "is not always optimized" by the balance and that inferring flow from it "is not a safe bet". The article reports the review's "central precondition" and Abuhamdeh's objection side by side, and labels Abuhamdeh as one author arguing a position. The Fong sentence is not printed because that paper was not read.
- **Is flow rare or common?** Abuhamdeh: rare by definition; the quadrant method counts 25% or more of daily experience. Printed as his criticism.
- **Kohoutkova's own framing.** The introduction says flow "can positively affect the quality of athlete's performance" and that martial arts cultivate mental and physical health. Neither claim is repeated.
- **Kohoutkova first name.** The PDF prints "Jindrisika"; the university record prints "Jindřiška". The article's source entry uses "Jindriska".

## 7. Claims cut and why

- *Cut:* any origin for the term "flow roll" or a first use. Nothing read gives one.
- *Cut:* "sometimes in the same breath" (of the two meanings being mixed in gyms). No page read mixes the round with the psychological state, and none mentions the psychology at all; the blurring is argued, not documented. The only documented slide is Evolve's two usages.
- *Cut:* any statement that flow rolling is safer, reduces injury, suits beginners, the injured or mismatched partners, or builds endurance. All appear on the gym pages; all are safety or performance claims.
- *Cut:* any statement that flow improves performance, wellbeing or retention. Peifer et al. report positive associations and say direction cannot be inferred; the article keeps only the caution.
- *Cut:* the proposed three meta-components (absorption, perceived demand-skill balance, enjoyment; Peifer & Engeser 2021) and the finding that a dynamic balance suits flow better than a static one (Baumann et al. 2016). Both second hand and not needed.
- *Cut:* Swann's finding that flow was perceived as controllable to some extent. Relevant, but it invites the reading that the state can be produced on demand, which the piece does not argue either way; the last section says only that this has not been tested for a flow roll.
- *Cut:* the earlier wording "nobody has shown that a room can schedule the second". Too broad: intervention studies exist (Goddard et al. 2021, not read). Replaced with a statement limited to flow rolls and to what was found.
- *Cut:* "a ninety-per-cent round". Invented figure.
- *Cut:* any link between Kohoutkova's "detached from the ego" and the ego piece. Tempting, unsupported.
- *Cut:* ADCC or other federations. Only the IBJJF book was searched, and the article says so.

## 8. Editorial notes

- The order is deliberate: the convention first in its teachers' words, then the absence of a rule, then the construct, its measurement problems, the two mat studies, and only then the argument. Moving the argument forward would let it borrow authority from the research.
- The piece treats the BJJ study carefully because it is the only one of its kind found and it is weak: an online convenience sample, a nine-item recalled-experience form, and no stated direction of effect. Do not add "rolling produced more flow than drilling" unless someone reads the table from the PDF by eye and the numbers hold up.
- Kellermann's study is about the Multi-Action Plan model, not a flow scale. "Flow-like" is the paper's word for Type 1 and should not be tightened to "flow".
- Consistency: the drilling piece (rehearsal against positional sparring) and the ego piece (a word shared with a research literature) are both referred to in the body and listed in `relatedSlugs`; both are registered in `index.ts`. `survival-first-and-the-order-things-are-learned-in` was read and is not linked: nothing in this piece bears on teaching order. No literature from those three pieces is repeated.
- Body copy is 1,798 words, two under the ceiling. Any restoration needs a matching cut.
