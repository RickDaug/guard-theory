## 3. Contradictory accounts — how each was handled

- **The body triangle.** UWW and ADCC accept it for full points; the IBJJF gives an advantage. Printed as a disagreement in §2 and §5 and recorded in `contestedNotes`. It is the article's best illustration that scoring tables encode preferences rather than facts.
- **Point values.** Three organisations, three values for the same position (4, 4, 3). Reported, not averaged.
- **Whether turtle is a position or a transition.** The rule books treat it as a transition; a good deal of coaching material treats it as a defensive system with its own attacks. The article takes the rule books' side and says why, but does not claim the alternative view is wrong — it says turtle "mostly cannot" attack.
- **Chest-first vs hooks-first teaching order.** A live disagreement in coaching. The article argues one side and states in `contestedNotes` that no outcome data supports it.

## 9. Fact-check status

**Passed, with the following cut:**

- *Cut:* every physiological statement, including anything about the body triangle beyond the observation that people tap to it and that the tap deserves release.
- *Cut:* the claim that back control is the highest-percentage position in grappling. The article says it is the highest-*value* position under the tables quoted, which is what those documents support, and then immediately undercuts the stronger reading with the Spanias submission-only result.
- *Cut:* any figure for how often a back take follows a turtle.
- *Cut:* a planned section on turtle offence (rolls, sit-outs, granby). Out of scope for a piece on taking the back, and it would have required sourcing the article does not have.
- *Cut:* the assertion that the IBJJF penalises the body triangle "because it is dangerous". The rule book gives it a lower score and gives no reason. Not inferred.
- *Cut:* the claim that the seat-belt side determines the outcome of a scramble. Softened to what it commits you to, which is a description rather than a prediction.
- *Cut:* everything from the injury literature. Same reasoning as the sibling articles: the commission forbids injury statistics and medical claims, and this transition is exactly the place where such literature would have been tempting.

## 10. Editorial notes
