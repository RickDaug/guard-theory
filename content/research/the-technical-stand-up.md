## 0. The brief's hard constraints

- No self-defence claims. Five of the six Grapplearts pages teach the stand-up primarily for self defence or MMA. None of that is carried over. The article says once that the material is addressed to those contexts and that only the grappling is used.
- No medical, injury-prevention or safety advice. The article contains none. Kesting's "you're going to get crushed" (Turkish getup comparison) and Yip's breakfall remarks were not used.
- No invented statistic, no ranking, no unverified record. Kesting describes Brandon Mullins as "a BJJ black belt world champion"; that was not repeated because no federation result was checked.
- Consistency with `seated-guard-and-supine-guard`, `why-the-underhook-decides-half-guard`, `the-bridge-and-the-hip-escape`, `base-posture-and-structure` and the Library entries `underhook-half-guard`, `sweeping-toward-the-missing-post`, `frames-versus-blocks`. The come-up order (elbow, hand, knee) and the reading of IBJJF 4.6.3 are theirs and are quoted or paraphrased, not changed.

## 3. Contradictions and gaps

- **BJJ Mental Models has no entry on standing up or wrestling up.** The brief suggested one might exist. The site's sitemap (256 URLs, fetched today) has no slug containing stand, wrestle or get-up. Only the seated-guard definition is used. No URL was guessed.
- **Names.** The brief mentions "technical get-up" and "standing up in base". Neither phrase appears in any page read, so the article does not assert that those names are in use.
- **ADCC is silent** on the bottom player coming to the feet, and on when a stand-up becomes a standing exchange. Stated in the article and in `contestedNotes`.
- **IBJJF pays the same two points either way** (4.6.3 sweep within three seconds; 4.1.1 takedown once 4.1.5 makes it standing combat). The article says so; it does not claim one is easier to earn.
- **Kesting's 2012 solo steps go elbow, then hand**; Mullins's full version is described starting from the hand post. No contradiction: the 2012 list starts from lying on the side.
- The ADCC page also lists match formats in which early periods carry no positive points. Not used; the article does not describe formats.

## 5. Claims cut and why

- Brandon Mullins as "world champion": Kesting's description, not verified against a federation record.
- "Technical get-up" / "standing up in base" as alternative names: not in any source read.
- That the movement is "usually" shown in the warm-up: no source for frequency. Replaced with what Yip and Kesting actually say (a solo drill; listed in a sample solo workout).
- Kesting on self defence and MMA (covering the head, kicking to make space, moving backwards, the fence stand-up, fighters named in the 2012 article): out of scope by the brief.
- Kesting's Turkish getup comparison ("you're going to get crushed") and Rob Biernacki's weight-bearing version: the first reads as a safety remark; the second is video-only detail.
- The X guard standup sweep and the butterfly and closed guard standups: described on the pages mainly by video or in a striking context; mentioned only through Kesting's one-line generalisation (A15).
- Kesting's remark that switching between standing and ground is tiring: a conditioning claim with no measurement.
- "A top player has to keep weight forward to prevent it": unsourced as a general statement; only Kesting's "will often drive forward" is paraphrased.
- Any statement about how often competitors stand up from guard: Kesting says it "happens ALL the time in competition"; not a count, not used.
